## 1. Purpose of the Full PRD

This Full Product Requirements Document defines the complete product behavior for **Tender Management v2**.

Tender Management v2 replaces the v1 document-set-centered tender module with a clean, Standard Tender Document-native orchestration layer. The module must support legally controlled tender creation, publication, supplier participation, clarifications, addenda, bid submission, closing, opening readiness, evaluation handoff, and contract handoff.

This PRD defines:

1. the module purpose;
2. the corrected ownership boundary;
3. functional scope;
4. lifecycle behavior;
5. STD Engine integration requirements;
6. supplier-facing requirements;
7. addendum and clarification behavior;
8. bid submission and closing behavior;
9. opening, evaluation, award, and contract handoff behavior;
10. Works-first implementation behavior;
11. audit and compliance requirements;
12. non-functional requirements;
13. acceptance criteria;
14. documentation and implementation gates.

This PRD does not define the final database schema, field-level model, permissions matrix, UI layouts, seed data, smoke tests, or implementation tickets. Those are covered by later artifacts in the required sequence.

## 2. Legal and Operational Basis

Tender Management v2 must be designed as a regulated public-sector control module. Its requirements are grounded in the following source families:

1. national public procurement law and regulation requiring fair, equitable, transparent, competitive, cost-effective, auditable, and procedurally controlled procurement;
2. PPRA standard tender documents and standard procurement formats;
3. rules and practices requiring tender documents to disclose submission requirements, deadlines, opening arrangements, validity periods, tender security requirements, evaluation criteria, and contract conditions;
4. e-procurement controls requiring electronic submission discipline, access control, confidentiality, system time authority, publication control, and electronic records;
5. World Bank procurement regulations and standard procurement documents as operational benchmarks for structured bidding/proposal documents, evaluation discipline, value-for-money controls, contract carry-forward, and record retention;
6. the platform’s accepted STD Engine architecture, which treats Standard Tender Documents as structured, versioned, governed source-of-truth models.

Tender Management v2 must implement these principles through system-enforced workflows, not manual instructions alone.

## 3. Product Problem

Tender Management v1 correctly recognized that tendering is a legal process, but it treated the Standard Tender Document primarily as a document set or uploaded/generated bundle. That approach creates unacceptable risk because downstream procurement behavior can diverge from the legal tender document.

The product problem is:

```text
The system must manage tender lifecycle events without allowing Tender Management to become an independent source of submission requirements, opening fields, evaluation criteria, or contract terms.
```

Tender Management v2 must prevent the following failures:

1. publication of a tender without a governed STD instance;
2. manual supplier submission checklist definition inside Tender Management;
3. manual opening register field definition inside Tender Management;
4. manual evaluation criteria injection during tender setup or evaluation handoff;
5. contract term divergence from tender terms;
6. silent post-publication changes;
7. addenda that change documents without regenerating affected downstream models;
8. supplier submission acceptance against requirements not derived from the STD;
9. opening using fields not derived from the STD;
10. evaluation using criteria not derived from the STD;
11. audit inability to prove end-to-end consistency from template to contract.

---

## 4. Product Goal

The product goal is to build Tender Management v2 as a legally defensible, STD-native orchestration module that ensures every tender lifecycle action is controlled, auditable, and traceable to the governing Standard Tender Document instance.

Tender Management v2 must ensure:

1. every tender originates from an authorized procurement package;
2. every tender binds to an active STD template version before publication;
3. every tender has a governed tender-specific STD instance;
4. every publication uses an immutable generated bundle from the STD Engine;
5. every supplier submission is validated against the STD-derived submission model;
6. every opening readiness handoff uses the STD-derived opening model;
7. every evaluation handoff uses the STD-derived evaluation model;
8. every contract handoff uses the STD-derived contract carry-forward model;
9. every material post-publication change is handled by addendum with impact analysis;
10. every material action is auditable.

---

## 5. Core Product Principle

Tender Management v2 must enforce this principle:

```text
Tender Management governs process. The STD Engine governs tender content, submission structure, opening structure, evaluation structure, and contract carry-forward.
```

This means:

1. Tender Management may coordinate tender creation and publication.
2. Tender Management may coordinate supplier participation and bid receipt.
3. Tender Management may coordinate clarifications, addenda, closing, and handoff.
4. Tender Management must not define or override the structured legal rules of the tender.
5. Tender Management must reject any workflow path that bypasses the STD Engine.

## 9. Tender Creation Requirements

### 9.1 Creation Preconditions

The system must allow tender creation only when all required preconditions are met:

1. source procurement package exists;
2. source procurement package is approved and released/authorized for tender;
3. package has procurement method and category;
4. package has procuring entity and fiscal context;
5. package has budget and planning lineage where required;
6. package is not already linked to an active tender unless retendering is authorized;
7. user has authority to create tender;
8. eligible STD template version exists for the method/category/contract type.

### 9.2 Creation Flow

Tender creation must follow this sequence:

1. user selects eligible released procurement package;
2. system displays package summary and eligibility status;
3. system queries STD Engine for eligible active template versions;
4. user selects STD template version and applicability profile where required;
5. system creates Tender Draft;
6. system creates or requests STD Engine to create tender STD instance;
7. system maps available package/tender data into STD parameters;
8. system generates initial readiness blockers;
9. system records audit event: Tender Created and STD Instance Bound.

### 9.3 Prohibited Creation Behaviors

Tender Management v2 must not allow:

1. free-form tender creation without package lineage;
2. tender creation without STD template version selection where publication is intended;
3. selection of inactive, draft, superseded, archived, or unapproved template versions for new tenders;
4. manual duplication of an existing tender without retender/supersession governance;
5. bypass of package method/category compatibility checks;
6. direct creation of supplier submission requirements during tender creation.

## 10. STD Binding Requirements

### 10.1 Binding Purpose

The STD binding proves which structured legal document model governs the tender.

Tender Management v2 must bind each tender to:

1. STD template family;
2. STD template version;
3. applicability profile, if required;
4. tender STD instance;
5. generated Bundle reference;
6. Document Submission Model reference;
7. Document Opening Model reference;
8. Document Evaluation Model reference;
9. Document Contract Model reference;
10. publication snapshot reference after publication.

### 10.2 Binding Rules

1. A tender may have only one controlling STD instance at a time.
2. The controlling STD instance must bind to exactly one template version.
3. A published tender must retain its original template version unless a governed supersession/migration/addendum path exists.
4. Template supersession must not silently alter published or active tenders.
5. STD binding must be immutable after publication except through governed addendum/supersession procedures.
6. Tender Management must consume STD readiness state, not compute document completeness independently.

---

## 11. STD Instance Completion Requirements

### 11.1 Completion Surface

Tender Management v2 must provide or link to an STD instance completion surface. This surface is not a free-form document editor. It must expose:

1. locked standard sections;
2. parameter-only sections;
3. structured editable tender-specific sections;
4. generated supplier forms;
5. structured schedules and BOQ where applicable;
6. section-bound supporting attachments;
7. derived submission checklist;
8. derived opening register preview;
9. derived evaluation model preview;
10. derived contract carry-forward preview;
11. validation blockers.

### 11.2 Completion Rules

1. Locked standard text must not be editable.
2. Parameterized text must accept values only through approved parameters.
3. Structured sections must be completed through defined forms/tables.
4. Supporting attachments must be bound to a section/component.
5. Generated sections must be regenerated by the STD Engine.
6. Manual edits to generated document outputs must not become source-of-truth data.
7. Completion must be auditable.

---

## 12. Publication Readiness Requirements

### 12.1 Readiness Validation

A tender may not be submitted for publication review until readiness validation passes.

Readiness must check:

1. package lineage valid;
2. tender status allows review submission;
3. STD template version active at selection time;
4. tender STD instance exists;
5. applicability profile selected where required;
6. required parameters complete and valid;
7. required structured sections complete;
8. required supporting attachments section-bound and versioned;
9. generated supplier-facing Bundle exists;
10. Document Submission Model generated and current;
11. Document Opening Model generated and current;
12. Document Evaluation Model generated and current;
13. Document Contract Model generated and current;
14. timeline valid;
15. supplier access rule valid;
16. eligibility/invitation rules valid where applicable;
17. no unresolved validation blockers;
18. required internal reviews completed where configured.

### 12.2 Readiness Output

The system must produce a readiness record showing:

1. pass/fail status;
2. each validation check;
3. blocker code;
4. blocker severity;
5. responsible owner/module;
6. remediation guidance;
7. timestamp;
8. actor/system that ran validation.

### 12.3 Blocker Behavior

Critical blockers must prevent submission for publication review. High blockers must prevent publication unless explicitly waived by authorized governance where legally permitted. Waivers must be exceptional, reasoned, audited, and visible to auditors.

## 13. Publication Review and Approval Requirements

### 13.1 Publication Review Flow

Tender Management v2 must support:

1. Draft;
2. Submit for Publication Review;
3. Under Publication Review;
4. Return for Correction;
5. Approve for Publication;
6. Publish.

The exact state model will be defined in the Governance & State Model.

### 13.2 Review Requirements

Publication reviewers must see:

1. tender summary;
2. package lineage;
3. STD template version and instance;
4. readiness result;
5. generated publication Bundle;
6. derived downstream model previews;
7. timeline;
8. supplier access configuration;
9. risk/compliance flags;
10. outstanding blockers;
11. previous review comments.

### 13.3 Approval Rules

1. Publication approval must be performed by an authorized role.
2. Creator and approver separation must be enforced where policy requires.
3. Approval must not alter tender content.
4. Approval must snapshot the approved readiness state.
5. Publication must use the approved generated Bundle and STD instance.
6. Publication must create an immutable publication record.
7. Publication must bind the exact published Bundle, DSM, DOM, DEM, and DCM output versions.

## 14. Publication Requirements

### 14.1 Publication Event

On publication, the system must:

1. change tender state to Published;
2. create publication record;
3. create or reference the STD Engine publication snapshot;
4. publish or expose the generated supplier-facing Bundle;
5. expose section-bound supporting attachments intended for suppliers;
6. activate supplier access according to visibility rules;
7. notify eligible suppliers or public channels as required;
8. create audit events;
9. lock published tender content and STD binding.

### 14.2 Publication Record

The publication record must capture:

1. tender code;
2. publication timestamp generated by server;
3. publication channel;
4. visibility mode;
5. generated Bundle reference and hash/checksum where available;
6. DSM, DOM, DEM, and DCM output references;
7. STD template version reference;
8. tender STD instance reference;
9. publication snapshot reference;
10. published metadata snapshot;
11. actor/system;
12. success/failure status;
13. failure reason where applicable.

### 14.3 Published Artifact Immutability

Published artifacts must be immutable. Later changes require addendum, cancellation/supersession, or retendering according to governance rules.

## 17. Clarification Requirements

### 17.1 Clarification Submission

Suppliers must be able to submit clarification requests during the allowed clarification window.

The request must capture:

1. tender reference;
2. supplier identity;
3. related section/clause/form/BOQ item where applicable;
4. question text;
5. attachments where permitted;
6. timestamp;
7. status.

### 17.2 Clarification Review

Internal users must review clarification requests and determine whether:

1. response is supplier-specific;
2. response is shared with all participants;
3. response is public;
4. question is invalid/out of scope;
5. clarification requires addendum.

### 17.3 Clarification-to-Addendum Escalation

If the response changes scope, requirements, deadlines, BOQ, submission obligations, evaluation, opening, or contract terms, the system must require addendum creation rather than ordinary clarification publication.

### 17.4 Clarification Publication

Official clarification responses must be approved before publication where policy requires. Publication must notify affected suppliers and create audit records.

## 18. Addendum Requirements

### 18.1 Addendum Purpose

An addendum is the only ordinary mechanism for material post-publication amendment of tender content.

Addenda may affect:

1. parameters;
2. deadlines;
3. Works Requirements;
4. BOQ items/quantities;
5. supplier forms;
6. submission requirements;
7. opening register model;
8. evaluation model;
9. contract carry-forward;
10. supporting attachments;
11. publication metadata.

### 18.2 Addendum Creation

Addendum creation must capture:

1. tender reference;
2. reason;
3. affected STD sections/components;
4. proposed changes;
5. impact category;
6. deadline impact;
7. supplier acknowledgement requirement;
8. legal/compliance review requirement;
9. generated revised outputs where applicable.

### 18.3 STD Impact Analysis

Tender Management v2 must request or consume STD Engine impact analysis before addendum approval where structured content is affected.

Impact analysis must identify:

1. affected parameters;
2. affected generated Bundle sections;
3. affected submission model;
4. affected opening model;
5. affected evaluation model;
6. affected contract carry-forward;
7. whether deadline extension is required or recommended;
8. whether supplier acknowledgement is required;
9. whether already submitted bids must be replaced, reaffirmed, or remain valid according to policy.

### 18.4 Addendum Approval and Issue

Addenda must be approved before issue. Issuance must:

1. create immutable addendum record;
2. regenerate affected outputs;
3. notify suppliers;
4. update supplier portal display;
5. require acknowledgement where configured;
6. update tender timeline where deadline changes are issued;
7. record audit events;
8. preserve superseded output references.

### 18.5 Prohibited Addendum Behavior

The system must not allow:

1. direct edit to published Bundle;
2. unapproved addendum issuance;
3. addendum that changes evaluation criteria without STD model regeneration;
4. BOQ change without versioning;
5. deadline change without audit and supplier notice;
6. hidden addendum visible only internally where supplier equality is affected.

## 19. Bid Draft and Submission Requirements

### 19.1 Bid Draft

Suppliers may prepare bid drafts where allowed. Drafts are not official submissions.

The system must:

1. validate draft structure against STD-derived submission model;
2. allow saving before deadline;
3. prevent internal users from seeing draft content;
4. allow supplier edits until final submission or deadline;
5. record draft activity where policy requires without exposing content.

### 19.2 Final Bid Submission

Final submission must:

1. occur before server-time submission deadline;
2. pass STD-derived required submission validation;
3. include all mandatory forms, declarations, securities, BOQ/pricing entries, and attachments required by the method;
4. create official bid submission record;
5. seal bid contents;
6. generate receipt;
7. timestamp submission using server time;
8. create audit event;
9. prevent unauthorized access before opening.

### 19.3 Replacement and Withdrawal

Where allowed by tender rules and before deadline:

1. supplier may replace submitted bid;
2. previous submission becomes superseded but retained;
3. supplier may withdraw bid;
4. withdrawal must be recorded and receipt issued;
5. replacement/withdrawal must be prohibited after deadline.

### 19.4 Late Submission

After server-time deadline, the system must reject submission attempts and record late attempt evidence. It must not create valid bid submission records after the deadline.

---

## 20. Works-Specific Bid Submission Requirements

For the initial Works implementation:

1. BOQ quantities must be set by the procuring entity and locked for suppliers.
2. Supplier may enter unit rates only for rate-editable BOQ items.
3. System computes line amounts as quantity multiplied by rate.
4. System computes BOQ total from line amounts and configured fixed sums.
5. Supplier cannot edit computed amounts.
6. Supplier cannot change BOQ item descriptions, units, quantities, bill structure, or provisional sums unless specifically allowed by the STD model.
7. Missing required rates must block submission.
8. Zero rates must be blocked unless the STD model explicitly permits them.
9. Negative rates must be blocked.
10. Arithmetic correction must not occur at submission.
11. Arithmetic correction must not occur at opening.
12. Arithmetic correction occurs only in Evaluation according to STD-derived rules.
13. Submitted BOQ snapshot must be sealed with the bid.
14. Contract value source must be corrected evaluated BOQ total after Evaluation.

## 21. Tender Closing Requirements

### 21.1 Closing Trigger

Tender closing must be based on authoritative server time.

The system must close the tender when:

1. server time is equal to or later than submission deadline;
2. tender is Published or equivalent active submission state;
3. no valid deadline extension is pending issuance;
4. no system-level hold applies.

Closing may be automatic or manually triggered by authorized users after deadline, but server time remains authoritative.

### 21.2 Closing Record

The closing record must capture:

1. tender reference;
2. submission deadline;
3. actual closure timestamp;
4. closure actor/system;
5. number of valid submissions, if policy permits recording/display;
6. number of withdrawn submissions;
7. number of late attempts;
8. addendum acknowledgement status where required;
9. closure outcome.

### 21.3 Post-Closing Behavior

After closing:

1. supplier submissions are blocked;
2. replacement and withdrawal are blocked unless legally permitted by exceptional procedure;
3. clarification submissions are blocked;
4. tender may proceed to opening readiness;
5. sealed bid contents remain inaccessible until lawful opening.

## 22. Opening Readiness Requirements

### 22.1 Opening Readiness Preconditions

Opening readiness may be prepared only when:

1. tender is closed;
2. valid submission set is finalized;
3. STD-derived opening model exists;
4. opening schedule is valid;
5. required addendum acknowledgements are satisfied or handled according to policy;
6. no unresolved blocking issue exists;
7. user/system has authority.

### 22.2 Opening Readiness Package

The package must include:

1. tender reference;
2. closing record;
3. sealed bid submission references;
4. supplier submission metadata allowed for opening;
5. STD-derived opening register model;
6. addendum history;
7. attendance/committee requirements where applicable;
8. audit references.

### 22.3 Opening Restrictions

Tender Management v2 must not open bids. It prepares opening readiness. The Bid Opening module executes opening according to its own governed process while consuming the STD-derived opening model.

## 23. Evaluation Handoff Requirements

### 23.1 Evaluation Handoff Preconditions

Evaluation handoff may occur only after:

1. formal opening is completed;
2. opening record is valid;
3. opened submissions are identified;
4. STD-derived evaluation model exists;
5. addendum history is attached;
6. no unresolved opening blocker prevents evaluation;
7. handoff is authorized.

### 23.2 Evaluation Handoff Package

The handoff package must include:

1. tender record;
2. procurement package lineage;
3. STD template version and instance;
4. generated Bundle reference;
5. derived submission model;
6. derived opening model;
7. derived evaluation model;
8. opening record;
9. opened submission references;
10. bid submission snapshots;
11. addenda and clarifications;
12. audit references.

### 23.3 Prohibited Evaluation Handoff Behavior

Tender Management v2 must not allow:

1. adding evaluation criteria at handoff;
2. removing STD-derived criteria;
3. changing weights or thresholds;
4. hiding criteria from audit;
5. passing incomplete or unbound evaluation model.

## 24. Award and Contract Handoff Requirements

Tender Management v2 is not the Award or Contract module, but it must preserve the data lineage needed for lawful award and contract formation.

### 24.1 Award Support

Tender Management v2 must make available:

1. tender and STD references;
2. publication and addendum history;
3. submitted/opened bid references;
4. evaluation model reference;
5. opening record reference;
6. supplier participation record;
7. audit trail.

### 24.2 Contract Support

Contract Management must receive or be able to reference:

## 25. Cancellation, Retendering, and Supersession Requirements

### 25.1 Cancellation

Tender cancellation is a high-risk governance event.

The system must require:

1. authorized actor;
2. reason;
3. current state validation;
4. legal/compliance review where required;
5. supplier notification where published;
6. immutable cancellation record;
7. audit event.

### 25.2 Retendering

Retendering must preserve lineage to the original tender and procurement package.

Retendering may create a new tender from:

1. original procurement package;
2. replacement package where planning requires adjustment;
3. previous tender reference;
4. selected active STD template version.

Retendering must not mutate the cancelled/failed tender.

### 25.3 Supersession

Where a tender must be replaced rather than amended, the system must create a superseding tender record with lineage. Superseded records remain available for audit.

## 40. Acceptance Criteria

Tender Management v2 is functionally acceptable only if the following are true.

### 40.1 Source-of-Truth Acceptance

1. Tender cannot be published without STD template version and tender STD instance.
2. Tender Management cannot define submission requirements independently.
3. Tender Management cannot define opening register fields independently.
4. Tender Management cannot define evaluation criteria independently.
5. Tender Management cannot define contract terms independently.
6. Generated document Bundle derives from STD instance.

### 40.2 Lifecycle Acceptance

1. Tender is created only from authorized package.
2. Publication review is blocked by STD readiness failures.
3. Published tender artifacts are immutable.
4. Addenda are governed and auditable.
5. Clarifications that change meaning escalate to addenda.
6. Tender closes using server time.
7. Opening readiness uses STD-derived opening model.
8. Evaluation handoff uses STD-derived evaluation model.
9. Contract handoff references STD-derived carry-forward.

### 40.3 Works Acceptance

1. Works tender uses Works STD template/version/profile.
2. BOQ is structured and first-class.
3. Supplier cannot edit quantities.
4. Supplier enters rates only.
5. Submission computes amounts but does not correct arithmetic.
6. Opening does not correct arithmetic.
7. Evaluation performs arithmetic correction.
8. Corrected evaluated BOQ total carries to contract.

### 40.4 Security Acceptance

1. Sealed bid contents are inaccessible before opening.
2. Supplier isolation is enforced.
3. Internal users cannot see supplier drafts.
4. Access controls are enforced server-side.
5. Audit records exist for material events.
6. Backend action availability governs legally meaningful actions.

### 40.5 Legacy Acceptance

1. v1 document-as-source paths are unavailable in v2.
2. Legacy data is archived/migrated according to approved plan.
3. No dual source of truth remains.
