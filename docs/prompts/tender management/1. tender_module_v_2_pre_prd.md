## 1. Purpose of This Pre-PRD

This Pre-PRD defines the corrected product direction for **Tender Management v2**.

Tender Management v1 was implemented around a document-set model in which Standard Tender Documents were effectively treated as uploaded, generated, or versioned tender document bundles. That model produced useful lifecycle, governance, audit, and bid-security patterns, but it placed the legal and computational source of truth in the wrong location.

Tender Management v2 corrects this by rebuilding the module as an **STD-native tender orchestration layer**.

The purpose of this artifact is to:

1. define why Tender Management v2 is required;
2. establish its legal and operational basis;
3. define the corrected module boundary;
4. define what the module owns and does not own;
5. establish the relationship between Tender Management and the STD Engine;
6. define the minimum lifecycle responsibilities of Tender Management v2;
7. identify critical governance, audit, legal, and integration risks;
8. define the documentation and implementation gates required before build.

This document is not the Full PRD. It does not yet define every field, screen, workflow, API, or test. It establishes the architectural and legal basis for those later artifacts.

## 2. External Source Basis

Tender Management v2 must be evidence-aligned and legally defensible. The following source families form the baseline external reference basis for this module:

1. **Kenya Public Procurement Regulatory Authority (PPRA)** standard tender documents and standard procurement formats.
2. **Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, 2015 (PPADA)**, including statutory requirements for fair, equitable, transparent, competitive, and cost-effective procurement; tender documents; tender opening; evaluation criteria; tender validity; supplier participation; and procurement records.
3. **Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Regulations, 2020**, including e-procurement, e-tendering, notices, submission, opening, confidentiality, publication, and procurement documentation controls.
4. **World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers**, including structured procurement documents, procurement planning, bidding/proposal processes, evaluation, award, contract management, record retention, and value-for-money procurement principles.
5. **World Bank Standard Procurement Documents and Contract Management Guidance**, used as operational benchmark material for structured procurement documents, contract carry-forward, and lifecycle discipline.
6. **System-specific STD Engine artifacts**, including the STD Engine governance, domain, generated output, readiness, security, audit, UI, seed, smoke, and Cursor implementation artifacts completed before this Tender Management v2 stream.

Where national legal rules and international best-practice guidance differ, the system must preserve jurisdictional compliance first and use international guidance to strengthen structure, auditability, and operational robustness.

## 3. Background and Problem Statement

### 3.1 Tender Management v1 Baseline

Tender Management v1 correctly recognized that public tendering is not ordinary CRUD. It included important controls such as:

1. tender creation from procurement packages;
2. publication governance;
3. document set versioning;
4. supplier access control;
5. bid confidentiality;
6. clarification and addendum workflows;
7. deadline closure;
8. opening readiness;
9. audit events;
10. role/state-based permissions.

These patterns remain valuable.

However, Tender Management v1 treated tender documents primarily as controlled document bundles rather than as structured legal and computational models. The document set became too central. This made it possible for the system to govern publication of tender documents while still failing to make the tender document structure the authoritative source for downstream processes.

### 3.2 Core Defect

The core defect was:

```text
Standard Tender Documents were treated as document attachments or generated bundles, rather than as structured, parameterized, versioned, legally controlled source-of-truth models.
```

This creates systemic risks:

1. supplier submission requirements may be manually configured outside the STD;
2. opening registers may be defined independently of the STD;
3. evaluation criteria may be duplicated or manually recreated;
4. contract terms may diverge from tender terms;
5. addenda may modify documents without regenerating affected downstream models;
6. audit reconstruction may prove what document was published but not prove that downstream actions followed the published STD structure.

### 3.3 Why Rebuild Is Preferred Over Refactor

Tender Management v2 is a rebuild rather than an in-place refactor because the v1 foundational assumption is wrong.

The v1 implementation can provide patterns and lessons, but its core data ownership model must not be preserved as the basis of v2. Attempting to retrofit the old document-set model risks dual sources of truth:

1. STD Engine as the new legal source of truth;
2. legacy Tender document/configuration objects as the old operational source of truth.

A dual-source model is unacceptable for public procurement. It creates audit ambiguity, hidden inconsistency, and legal exposure.

Tender Management v2 must therefore be designed cleanly from the start as an STD-native orchestration module.

## 4. Core Product Principle

Tender Management v2 must enforce the following principle:

```text
No tender may be published, submitted to, opened, evaluated, awarded, or converted to contract unless its controlling rules are derived from a governed Standard Tender Document instance.
```

This means:

1. Tender Management does not define evaluation criteria.
2. Tender Management does not define supplier submission requirements.
3. Tender Management does not define opening register fields.
4. Tender Management does not define contract carry-forward terms.
5. Tender Management does not treat uploaded files as the source of legal tender structure.
6. Tender Management orchestrates lifecycle, publication, supplier participation, submission receipt, closing, addenda issuance, and handoff using STD Engine outputs.

## 5. Legal and Operational Design Basis

### 5.1 Public Procurement Integrity

Public procurement must be fair, equitable, transparent, competitive, cost-effective, auditable, and resistant to arbitrary discretion. Tender Management is the point where an internal procurement package becomes an external market-facing legal process. Its design must therefore minimize ambiguity, unauthorized discretion, and undocumented changes.

### 5.2 Tender Documents as Governing Instruments

Tender documents are not ordinary attachments. They define:

1. who may participate;
2. what suppliers must submit;
3. where, when, and how tenders are submitted;
4. tender validity and security requirements;
5. opening information;
6. evaluation criteria and procedures;
7. award and contract formation requirements;
8. contract terms and special conditions.

Tender Management v2 must therefore ensure that these rules are consumed structurally from the STD Engine.

### 5.3 Standard Tender Documents and Structured Control

PPRA standard tender documents and World Bank standard procurement documents commonly distinguish between fixed standard text, tender-specific data, forms, evaluation requirements, schedules, and contract conditions. The system must respect this by using structured templates, parameters, forms, schedules, criteria, and carry-forward mappings rather than uncontrolled document editing.

### 5.4 E-Procurement Discipline

Electronic procurement requires stronger, not weaker, controls. The system must enforce:

1. authoritative server time;
2. sealed bid confidentiality;
3. equal supplier access to published information;
4. immutable publication snapshots;
5. auditable addenda;
6. role/state-based permissions;
7. no silent post-publication change;
8. controlled handoff to opening, evaluation, award, and contract.

## 6. Scope Position of Tender Management v2

Tender Management v2 is the controlled orchestration layer between Procurement Planning and downstream procurement execution.

Lifecycle position:

```text
Procurement Planning
→ STD Engine Template Selection and Tender STD Instance
→ Tender Management v2
→ Supplier Portal / Bid Submission
→ Bid Opening
→ Evaluation
→ Award
→ Contract Management
→ Execution / Inspection / Audit
```

Tender Management v2 is not the STD Engine. It consumes the STD Engine.

Tender Management v2 is not the Evaluation module. It hands off to Evaluation.

Tender Management v2 is not Contract Management. It passes forward STD-derived contract inputs.

Tender Management v2 is not Supplier Management. It consumes eligibility and supplier identity services.

## 7. Module Objectives

Tender Management v2 must achieve the following objectives.

### 7.1 Legal Objectives

1. Ensure every tender is linked to an authorized procurement package.
2. Ensure every published tender is linked to an active STD template version and tender STD instance.
3. Preserve the exact STD template version and instance used for each tender.
4. Prevent post-publication silent modification.
5. Ensure all material changes are issued through governed addenda.
6. Preserve complete audit evidence of tender lifecycle events.
7. Maintain equality of information among suppliers.
8. Protect sealed bid confidentiality until lawful opening.

### 7.2 Operational Objectives

1. Provide an internal Tender Workbench for managing tender lifecycle queues.
2. Provide an STD instance completion and readiness surface rather than a free-form document builder.
3. Coordinate publication and supplier access.
4. Manage clarifications and addenda.
5. Coordinate bid submission windows and supplier participation.
6. Enforce deadline closure using server time.
7. Prepare opening readiness using the STD-derived opening model.
8. Provide clean handoff to Evaluation using the STD-derived evaluation model.
9. Provide clean handoff to Contract Management using the STD-derived contract carry-forward model.

### 7.3 Architectural Objectives

1. Eliminate Tender Management as a source of submission, opening, evaluation, or contract rules.
2. Eliminate uncontrolled document upload as the source of tender structure.
3. Preserve only generated bundles, section-bound supporting attachments, and immutable published artifacts as outputs.
4. Ensure all cross-module interaction occurs through services or controlled interfaces, not direct database writes.
5. Support method-specific behavior through STD Engine templates and profiles.

## 11. Anti-Goals

Tender Management v2 must explicitly avoid the following.

1. No free-form tender creation detached from Procurement Planning.
2. No tender publication without active STD template version and tender STD instance.
3. No manual supplier submission requirement configuration inside Tender Management.
4. No manual opening register field configuration inside Tender Management.
5. No manual evaluation criteria injection inside Tender Management.
6. No contract term editing inside Tender Management.
7. No supplier-facing legal tender structure based solely on uploaded files.
8. No direct edit to published tender content.
9. No silent deadline, requirement, evaluation, BOQ, or contract changes after publication.
10. No sealed bid access before lawful opening.
11. No bypass of Supplier Management eligibility checks.
12. No cross-module direct database writes.
13. No raw technical objects exposed as primary user navigation.

## 12. Tender Lifecycle Concept

Tender Management v2 must support, at minimum, the following lifecycle concept.

```text
Package Released to Tender
→ Tender Draft Created
→ STD Template Version Selected
→ Tender STD Instance Created
→ STD Parameters / Sections Completed
→ STD Readiness Validated
→ Publication Review Submitted
→ Publication Approved
→ Tender Published
→ Supplier Participation / Clarifications / Addenda
→ Submission Deadline Reached
→ Tender Closed
→ Opening Readiness Prepared
→ Bid Opening Completed
→ Evaluation Handoff Completed
→ Award / Contract Handoff
```

Detailed state transitions will be defined in the Governance & State Model artifact.

## 13. STD-Native Publication Readiness

Tender Management v2 must not use a generic “document set complete” flag as its publication basis.

Publication readiness must depend on STD Engine outputs.

A tender may not be submitted for publication review unless all required readiness checks pass:

1. procurement package lineage is valid;
2. procurement method is compatible with selected STD template version;
3. STD template version is active;
4. tender STD instance exists;
5. applicability profile is selected where required;
6. required parameters are complete;
7. required structured sections are complete;
8. supporting attachments are section-bound and versioned;
9. generated document bundle exists;
10. supplier submission model is generated;
11. opening register model is generated;
12. evaluation model is generated;
13. contract carry-forward model is generated;
14. unresolved validation blockers are absent;
15. governance approvals required for publication are satisfied.

## 14. Method-Specific Handling

Tender Management v2 must be method-aware but not method-defining.

Procurement method behavior must be driven by STD Engine template/version/profile outputs.

Examples:

| Method / Category | STD-Driven Differences |
|---|---|
| Works | BOQ, quantities fixed by procuring entity, supplier rates only, arithmetic correction at evaluation, corrected evaluated BOQ total carried to contract. |
| Goods | Specifications, delivery schedule, price schedule, compliance requirements, warranty and after-sales terms. |
| Consultancy / RFP | Technical and financial proposal separation, method-specific evaluation such as QCBS/QBS/FBS/LCS/CQS/SSS, financial opening controls, negotiation where permitted. |
| RFQ | Simplified quotation structure, restricted invitation, quotation comparison, shorter timelines where lawful. |
| Non-consulting services | Service-level requirements, manpower/equipment schedules, performance standards, contract service obligations. |
| Disposal | Disposal notice, eligibility, bidding/auction rules, asset description, sale conditions. |
| Framework agreements | Framework establishment or call-off structure, supplier ranking or mini-competition rules, validity and call-off terms. |

Tender Management v2 must not hard-code method logic where the STD Engine should provide it.

## 15. Works as Initial Implementation Method

The first end-to-end implementation should use **Works** as the baseline method/category because it exposes the highest-risk structural requirements:

1. structured Works Requirements;
2. section-bound drawings/specifications/site information;
3. BOQ as a first-class structured pricing object;
4. fixed procuring-entity quantities;
5. supplier-entered rates;
6. computed amounts and totals;
7. arithmetic correction only during evaluation;
8. opening register total display without premature evaluation correction;
9. corrected evaluated BOQ total as contract price source;
10. contract carry-forward from SCC, Works Requirements, BOQ, and contract forms.

If Works is implemented correctly, later methods can reuse the orchestration pattern while extending method-specific STD validation packs.

## 16. Addendum Principle

Post-publication changes must never be silent.

Tender Management v2 must treat addenda as governed legal events. An addendum may affect:

1. tender deadlines;
2. STD parameters;
3. Works Requirements;
4. BOQ quantities or items;
5. supplier forms;
6. submission requirements;
7. opening register fields;
8. evaluation model;
9. contract carry-forward.

Any addendum with structural impact must trigger STD Engine impact analysis and regeneration of affected outputs. Tender Management v2 must coordinate approval, issuance, supplier notification, acknowledgement where required, and audit recording.

---

## 17. Clarification Principle

Clarifications must preserve supplier equality of information.

Tender Management v2 must support supplier questions and official responses, but it must distinguish between:

1. clarification that explains existing tender content;
2. clarification that materially changes tender content.

If a clarification changes meaning, scope, requirements, pricing, evaluation, submission, or contract terms, it must be escalated into an addendum path.

---

## 18. Bid Submission Principle

Tender Management v2 must enforce submission through STD-derived submission models.

A supplier submission must be validated against the controlling tender STD instance and submission model.

For Works, the first implementation must enforce:

1. required forms from the STD;
2. required declarations from the STD;
3. tender security rules from STD parameters;
4. BOQ rate entry only by supplier;
5. no supplier quantity edits;
6. no arithmetic correction at submission;
7. sealed bid record after submission;
8. receipt generation with timestamp and submission reference;
9. no late submission acceptance after server-time deadline.

---

## 19. Opening Readiness Principle

Opening readiness must derive from the STD opening register model.

Tender Management v2 must not determine opening fields independently. It must prepare the opening handoff using:

1. tender metadata;
2. supplier submission metadata;
3. STD-derived opening register schema;
4. sealed submission references;
5. deadline closure record;
6. addendum acknowledgement status where required.

For Works, opening must not perform arithmetic correction. Opening may record or display the submitted total tender price or BOQ total according to the STD-derived opening model.

---

## 20. Evaluation Handoff Principle

Evaluation must receive the STD-derived evaluation model.

Tender Management v2 must not allow evaluation criteria, responsiveness requirements, qualification criteria, or scoring logic to be manually injected at handoff.

The Evaluation module may execute evaluation, record findings, and perform method-permitted calculations. It must not redefine the criteria set by the tender STD instance.

For Works, arithmetic correction must occur only at evaluation according to the STD-derived rules. The corrected evaluated BOQ total must become the evaluated price source for award and contract carry-forward.

---

## 21. Contract Handoff Principle

Contract Management must receive contract carry-forward values from the STD Engine and award outcome.

Tender Management v2 must not author contract terms. It must pass forward:

1. tender identity;
2. tender STD instance reference;
3. generated contract carry-forward model reference;
4. addendum history;
5. award result reference;
6. corrected evaluated price where applicable;
7. final BOQ or schedule reference where applicable.

Contract creation must be blocked if proposed contract terms conflict with the controlling tender STD instance, addenda, award, or corrected evaluated values.

---

## 22. Supplier Access and Equality of Information

Tender Management v2 must preserve equal access to official tender information.

Supplier-facing information must be limited to:

1. published tender notice and metadata;
2. generated STD document bundle;
3. section-bound supporting attachments intended for supplier access;
4. clarification responses intended for that visibility level;
5. issued addenda;
6. supplier-specific participation and submission records;
7. official notifications and receipts.

Supplier-facing information must not expose:

1. internal estimates unless formally published;
2. internal review comments;
3. other suppliers’ bid contents;
4. internal audit logs;
5. evaluation notes before lawful publication;
6. hidden criteria or unpublished rules.

## 24. Data Retention and Immutability Principles

Tender Management v2 must preserve legally significant records.

1. Published tender artifacts are immutable.
2. Tender STD instance publication snapshots are immutable.
3. Generated document bundles are immutable after publication.
4. Addenda create new issued artifacts; they do not overwrite prior publication artifacts.
5. Superseded outputs must remain available for audit.
6. Submitted bids and receipts must remain tamper-evident.
7. Tender cancellation does not delete tender history.
8. Template supersession does not alter existing tenders.

## 28. Primary Risks and Required Mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---:|---|
| Legacy document logic remains active | Critical | Rebuild v2 cleanly; freeze v1; prohibit legacy document-set-as-source behavior. |
| STD Engine and Tender Management both define rules | Critical | Explicit integration contract; server-side validation; smoke tests for no manual rule injection. |
| Downstream modules bypass STD outputs | Critical | Cross-module validation framework and method-specific validation packs. |
| Published tender modified silently | Critical | Immutable publication snapshot; addendum-only changes; audit events. |
| Evaluation criteria diverge from tender | Critical | Evaluation model generated by STD; Evaluation cannot add hidden criteria. |
| Contract diverges from tender terms | Critical | Contract generation blocked unless carry-forward matches STD/award values. |
| Supplier sees unequal or unpublished information | High | Visibility controls, addendum distribution, clarification visibility rules. |
| Bid content exposed before opening | Critical | Sealed bid access guard; server-side file/content protection. |
| BOQ arithmetic correction performed too early | High | Enforce evaluation-stage-only correction for Works. |
| Migration from v1 creates contaminated data | High | Archive v1; migrate only eligible test/demo tenders; require fresh STD binding for v2. |
| UI reintroduces upload-driven behavior | High | STD instance completion surface; section-bound attachments only. |
