**Demand Intake and Approval — Simplified UX Brief**

**1\. What It Is**

Demand Intake and Approval is the module where departments formally request goods, works, services, or consultancy needs before procurement planning begins.

It answers:

What is needed?

Why is it needed?

Who requested it?

Is it legitimate and justified?

Can it be funded?

Has it been approved for procurement planning?

Demand Intake is not procurement planning. It is the controlled entry point for departmental needs.

The module converts a departmental request into an approved, budget-linked demand that can later be added to an active procurement plan and packaged for tendering.

Lifecycle position:

Strategy

→ Budget

→ Demand Intake and Approval

→ Procurement Planning

→ Tender Management

→ Award

→ Contract

**2\. Model**

A Demand represents a formal need raised by a department or business unit.

Core structure:

Demand Request

└── Demand Items

└── Department justification

└── Estimated value

└── Finance / budget validation

└── Strategy alignment

└── Supporting documents

└── Approval evidence

**Main Demand Header Data**

| **Field** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| Demand title | Human-readable need title |
| Requesting department | Department or unit raising the demand |
| Requestor | User creating the demand |
| Fiscal year | Year in which procurement is needed |
| Procurement category | Goods, Works, Services, Consultancy |
| Estimated value | Expected procurement value |
| Priority | Normal, High, Emergency |
| Need-by date | Required delivery/contract start date |
| Justification | Business reason for the demand |
| Attachments | Specifications, needs assessment, memos, supporting evidence |
| Strategy link | Linked by Finance / Planning, not normally by the requestor |
| Budget line | Linked by Finance / Budget, not normally by the requestor |
| Funding status | Not assessed, Available, Reserved, Insufficient |
| Demand status | Draft, Submitted, Returned, Funding Review, Final Review, Approved, Rejected, Cancelled |

**Demand Item Data**

| **Field** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- |
| Item / work description | What is being requested |
| Quantity / scope | Quantity or scope description |
| Unit estimate | Estimated unit value if applicable |
| Total estimate | Estimated item total |
| Category | Goods, Works, Services, Consultancy |
| Delivery location | Where the item/service/work is needed |
| Technical notes | Requirements or specifications |
| Supporting attachments | Specifications, drawings, memos, needs assessment, etc. |

**3\. Workflow, Approvals, and Roles**

Use a simple workflow with clear ownership.

Draft

→ Submitted

→ Returned / Funding Review

→ Final Review

→ Approved / Rejected

**Standard Workflow**

| **Step** | **Actor** | **Action** | **Result** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1   | Requestor / Department User | Create demand and describe the need | Draft demand exists |
| 2   | Requestor | Add items, estimated value, justification, need-by date, and attachments | Demand is complete enough for review |
| 3   | Requestor | Submit demand | Status: Submitted |
| 4   | Department Approver | Confirm the need is legitimate, justified, and department-owned | Approved forward, returned, or rejected |
| 5   | Finance / Budget Reviewer | Link budget line, confirm available funding, confirm strategy alignment, and reserve funds if required | Funding status updated |
| 6   | Procurement Approval Authority | Confirm procurement category, completeness, and approval for procurement planning | Approved or returned/rejected |
| 7   | System | Lock approved demand baseline, record approval evidence, and expose demand to Procurement Planning | Approved demand available for planning |

**Simplified Role Set**

| **Role** | **Responsibility** |
| --- | --- |
| Requestor | Creates and submits the demand |
| Department Approver | Confirms the department need and justification |
| Finance / Budget Reviewer | Links strategy and budget, checks funding, and records funding decision |
| Procurement Approval Authority | Performs final procurement-facing approval and authorizes release to planning |
| System | Locks baseline, records evidence, reserves budget where applicable, and exposes approved demand to Planning |

**Important Role Rule**

The requestor should not be expected to know the correct strategy objective or budget line.

The requestor should provide:

what is needed

why it is needed

when it is needed

estimated value

supporting documents

department ownership

Finance / Budget should provide:

budget line

funding source

funding availability

strategy/programme/objective alignment

reservation decision

Procurement Approval Authority should provide:

final approval for procurement planning

procurement category validation

readiness for Planning handoff

**4\. Budget Impact of Final Demand Approval**

Final demand approval has a direct effect on the linked budget.

Once Finance confirms funding and the Procurement Approval Authority gives final approval, the system should create or confirm a budget reservation for the approved demand.

**Budget Effect**

Available budget

→ Reserved for approved demand

→ Carried into Procurement Planning

→ Later attached to package

→ Later converted to commitment after award/contract

**What Approval Means Financially**

Final approval means:

The need is accepted.

The budget line is confirmed.

The estimated amount is reserved or funding-cleared.

The demand can proceed to Procurement Planning.

The approved demand baseline is locked for audit.

**Example**

Budget line:

Health Infrastructure Renovation

Approved amount: KES 100,000,000

Demand:

District Hospital Renovation Works

Estimated value: KES 98,000,000

After final demand approval:

Reserved amount: KES 98,000,000

Available balance: KES 2,000,000

Funding status: Reserved

**Controls**

The system should prevent final approval if:

no budget line is linked;

funding is insufficient;

strategy alignment is missing where required;

estimated value exceeds available budget;

mandatory attachments are missing;

department approval is incomplete.

If an exception is allowed, it must be explicit:

Approved with funding exception

Approved pending supplementary budget

Approved subject to budget revision

Such exceptions must be visible to Procurement Planning.

**5\. Linkages and Handoffs**

Demand Intake connects department needs to funding, strategy, and procurement planning.

**Upstream Links**

| **Module** | **Link** |
| --- | --- |
| Strategy Management | Finance / Planning links the demand to a strategic plan, programme, objective, target, or initiative |
| Budget Management | Finance / Budget links the demand to a budget line or funding source |
| User Department / Business Unit | Demand originates from a department need |

**Downstream Handoff**

Once approved, a demand becomes available in Procurement Planning.

Approved Demand

→ Add to Active Procurement Plan

→ Create Procurement Package

→ Release Package to Tender Management

The approved demand handoff to Planning must include:

demand title

requesting department

category

estimated value

budget line

funding status

reserved amount, if applicable

strategy link

approved demand items

attachments/specifications

approval evidence

**Handoff Rule**

Approved demands must be locked for audit before Planning consumes them.

Planning may reference the approved demand, but should not silently change the original approved need.

Changes after final approval require one of:

demand revision

approved correction

return/reopen process

**6\. Required User Experience**

The UI should be simple and task-oriented.

**Main Surfaces**

Demand Workbench

Create Demand Wizard

Demand Detail

Approvals Queue

Funding Review Queue

Approved Demands

Evidence Drawer

**Demand Workbench**

Purpose:

Show my drafts, submitted demands, returned demands, pending approvals, funding review items, and approved demands.

Recommended queues:

My Drafts

Returned to Me

Department Review

Funding Review

Final Review

Approved

Rejected / Cancelled

**Create Demand Wizard**

Use a guided flow:

Step 1: Describe Need

Step 2: Add Items / Scope

Step 3: Add Justification and Attachments

Step 4: Review and Submit

Do not ask the requestor to select budget lines or strategy objectives during normal demand creation.

Instead, after submission, Finance/Budget completes:

strategy alignment

budget line linkage

funding validation

reservation decision

**Demand Detail**

Demand Detail should answer:

What is being requested?

Why is it needed?

Who requested it?

What is the estimated value?

Has the department approved it?

Has Finance linked budget and strategy?

Is funding available or reserved?

Who needs to approve it next?

What is the next action?

Recommended layout:

Top: Demand title, department, status, estimated value

Main: Demand summary, items, justification, attachments

Right: Funding, strategy alignment, approval status, next action

Bottom/Drawer: Evidence and history

**Approval UX**

Approval screens should show:

what is requested

who requested it

estimated value

justification

attachments

department decision

budget/funding status

strategy alignment

approval history

next decision

Approver actions:

Approve

Return for Correction

Reject

Request Clarification

View Evidence

**Funding Review UX**

Finance/Budget users should see:

demand value

suggested fiscal year

requesting department

available budget lines

strategy/programme/objective options

available balance

amount to reserve

result after reservation

Finance actions:

Link Budget Line

Link Strategy Objective

Confirm Funding Available

Reserve Funding

Mark Funding Insufficient

Return for Clarification

Use simple labels for requestors and approvers:

Budget linked

Funding available

Funding reserved

Insufficient funds

Funding not linked

Strategy linked

Strategy alignment missing

Do not expose budget ledger mechanics to ordinary demand users.

**Evidence UX**

Evidence is available on request, not shown as default clutter.

Evidence should include:

submission record

department approval

budget validation

strategy alignment

funding reservation

final approval decision

attachments

revision history

audit events

**Key UX Rules**

Requestors create needs; they do not perform finance coding.

Finance/Budget links budget and strategy.

Final Procurement Approval Authority combines procurement review and final approval.

Final approval should reserve or confirm funding and expose the approved demand to Procurement Planning.

Approved demands should clearly move to Procurement Planning.

Technical records, handoff IDs, and audit details should remain behind View Evidence.

**Final Product Rule**

Demand Intake and Approval succeeds if a normal department user can describe a need, add items, justify the request, attach supporting documents, and submit it without needing to understand budget lines, strategy hierarchy, procurement packages, or tender mechanics.

Finance/Budget then links the demand to strategy and funding.

Final approval confirms the demand is legitimate, funded or funding-cleared, locked for audit, and ready for Procurement Planning.
