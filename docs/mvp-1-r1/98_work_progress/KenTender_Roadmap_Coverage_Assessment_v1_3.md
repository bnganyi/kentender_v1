## 1. Purpose and authority

This assessment compares the client roadmap with KenTender’s controlled requirements and identifies the next work. It is **directional, not normative**. Approved owner documents define permitted implementation; this assessment does not approve a proposed successor, change a statutory rule, expand a release gate or establish production readiness.

Status is separated into requirements, coordinated interfaces, implementation and production release. An approved requirements document is not evidence of completed code, integration tests, legal clearance or deployment. The current baseline register and documentation control register record those statuses separately.

## 5. Retained programme boundaries and variances

### 5.1 Public and supplier ownership

- **Start bid** creates the candidate arrangement and workspace atomically; there is no passive Follow Tender or standalone declaration-of-interest workflow.
- Tenders owns pre-bid questions, published answers, addenda and candidate-notice evidence. BDS displays their authorised projections.
- Evaluation owns post-opening bid-specific clarification and correspondence. It is separate from public pre-bid Q&A and never changes the submitted bid.
- Bid Opening owns post-close disclosure. Automatic submission close never reveals sealed bidder identities, prices or content.
- Supplier account registration and uploaded evidence never establish eligibility. Deterministic Evaluation checks and recorded evidence judgments apply the issued tender rules.

### 5.2 Automation principle

KenTender automatically applies published rules, comparisons and calculations and generates results/report content. Committee members review results, resolve evidence or interpretation requiring judgment and personally sign the report. No universal repetition or individual re-entry of completed system checks is required. MVP 1 uses explicit rules; AI document interpretation does not decide compliance or rejection. Uncertainty remains Needs review, not an automatic failure.

### 5.3 Production readiness

Production submission and downstream use require the applicable approved operating profile and verification for electronic signing, authoritative time, custody separation, secure storage, audit, backup, recovery, incidents and supplier receipts. Requirements approval, synthetic tests and architecture decisions do not themselves satisfy those gates. This assessment introduces no new gate and waives none of the existing ones.

### Gate 2 — Complete the Open Tender decision chain

1. **Bid Opening — requirements approved:** BOP v0.10 with approved PRC v0.9 and the shared Trust boundary. Implementation/integration evidence remains required.
2. **Bid Evaluation — requirements approved:** EVL v0.4 including R1–R3. Review/admit the coordinated owner successors in §4 and implement their bindings without repeating committee work.
3. **Award — next requirements work:** begin at the Head of Procurement’s receipt of the exact signed Evaluation report. First agree a short high-level contract, then produce the detailed requirements, actor/state fixtures, interaction map and acceptance evidence.

Award’s proposed coverage is professional opinion; Accounting Officer decision and reasons; return/correction and no-award/cancellation interfaces; successful and unsuccessful bidder notifications and delivery evidence; applicable standstill/review/suspension handling; debrief requests; award acceptance and relevant securities; and the exact contract handoff. Its legal mapping must establish the applicable obligations and timing. This list does not assert that every item is a new screen, mandatory human step or independent approval.

Award must consume EVL’s versioned report and preserve its authorship and signatures. Define how pre-decision returns differ from corrections after a decision, and supply the authoritative downstream-decision fact EVL needs. Do not invent a generic Tender Committee approval stage from the roadmap. Include it only if the governing requirements establish its necessity and owner.

### Gate 3 — Expand supplier and procurement coverage

4. **Supplier Management and Prequalification:** separate, purpose-limited requirements, with method-specific lists and correction/review rules.
5. **Controlled method/product releases:** RFQ/Restricted/Direct; RFP; Works; other Services. Order individual releases by an explicit programme decision and readiness, not by adding generic options to the first product.
6. Re-run the cross-document interface review whenever a method/product is admitted.

### Later phases

Contract Management follows a stable Award-to-contract handoff. IFMIS, Inventory, Stores and Assets follow with explicit real owner/interfaces. They remain visible programme work; this revision does not assign delivery dates or claim that they are part of the current release.

## 7. Review rule to prevent drift

A module is not complete merely because its own document is internally consistent. Its preceding and following owner interfaces must be explicit, and implementation status must reflect actual evidence.

| Required interface fact | Question to close |
|---|---|
| Upstream source | Which approved immutable record/version authorises the work? |
| Downstream result | What exact event or artifact is delivered, and what may its recipient do? |
| Actor and scope | Which current responsibility, appointment and eligibility permit each action/read? |
| Supplier/public effect | What can each external actor see, submit, receive or challenge? |
| State and events | Which action commits a business decision, versus saving or displaying information? |
| Evidence | What preserves actor, time, version, reasons, source and delivery? |
| Failure/recovery | What happens on replay, outage, stale source, missing integration, closure races or partial delivery? |
| Design and interaction | Does each required actor/state have exact content, actions and a named next holder? |
| Acceptance and fixture | Can each branch be reproduced without inventing sources, privileges or outcomes? |
| Approval and release | Is each document approved or proposed, and which implementation/test/release evidence actually exists? |

For each change, trace the rule through command, task creation/clearing, screen, scenario and acceptance case. Update the owning contract and both registers together. Preserve source versions and historical approvals; never treat a consumer’s approval as silent approval of a pending producer amendment.
