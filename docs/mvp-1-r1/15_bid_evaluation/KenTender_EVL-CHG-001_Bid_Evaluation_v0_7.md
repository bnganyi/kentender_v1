## 1. The MVP contract

**KenTender evaluates bids against the published rules automatically. Committee members resolve matters requiring judgment, review the conclusions and personally sign the report. They do not repeat completed automatic checks.**

MVP 1 covers Open Tender for straightforward IT Goods: one lot, KES, fixed price, published eligibility and mandatory technical requirements, and the lowest-evaluated-responsive method. Reservation conditions are inherited from the published tender. There are no weights, scores, editable criteria or AI decisions.

The ordinary journey is:

1. **Prepare the committee.** The Accounting Officer appoints the members. Each member declares conflicts and accepts confidentiality. This can happen before opening; it gives no access to sealed bids.
2. **Check the bids.** After a completed opening, the system receives the unchanged bids and applies the published rules. No receipt approval or “Start evaluation” click is required.
3. **Resolve what needs attention.** Members review evidence, discuss differences and obtain permitted written clarification. The system updates the affected results and comparison.
4. **Review and sign the report.** The system prepares the report. Members review the same version and sign personally. The last required signature sends it to the Head of Procurement automatically.

Professional opinion, the Accounting Officer's award decision, supplier award notifications and contract formation are downstream responsibilities. Evaluation produces a recommendation, never an award. Proceedings records the committee's actual work inside Evaluation; it adds no separate meeting workspace or approval cycle.

## 2. Authority and boundaries

### 2.1 Legal basis and product choices

The 2022 revised Act provides for an appointed evaluation committee (§46), responsiveness (§79), published evaluation criteria and member-signed reports (§80), written clarification that does not change tender terms (§81), preservation of the submitted/read-out sum for this ordinary Open Tender scope (§82), due diligence (§83), and a separate professional opinion (§84). These are the legal anchors; automated checking is the approved KenTender implementation choice, not a claim of statutory permission for autonomous award.

Source: [Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, revised 2022](https://pppkenya.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Public-Procurement-and-Asset-Disposal-Act-33-of-2015-Revised-2022.pdf), especially §§46 and 79–84. The [PPRA standard-document catalogue](https://ppra.go.ke/standard-tender-documents/) identifies a Goods/Works evaluation-report format. Its complete downloadable content was not successfully retrieved in this review; exact output-format reconciliation remains recorded in §12, rather than falsely claimed complete.

Existing LAW-V-001 retains current-law verification, including the effect of litigation concerning the 2020 Regulations. This document does not settle that issue or reproduce disputed regulation-only voting rules. The following are explicit **MVP product procedures**: all members of the current eligible roster participate in collective decision sessions; no chair override or automatic majority vote; members may sign an accurate report while recording disagreement. These procedures do not assert a statutory quorum formula.

### 2.2 What stays outside this module

- Bid Opening remains a separate public procedural event. Its observations are evidence to examine, not automatic evaluation findings.
- Tender criteria, reservation conditions, original responses and prices cannot be edited here. An account marked Active or an attachment accepted by BDS does not establish qualification.
- No external KRA, AGPO, banking or company verification is presumed. MVP members record evidence checks; future integrations must provide an authoritative result before an automatic check may rely on them.
- No negotiation, new bid, price correction, invented tie-break, retrospective waiver or supplier rejection notice is created here.
- Production signing-provider and custody implementation remain deferred under TRUST-ADR-001. They are shared infrastructure work, not extra Evaluation screens or design prerequisites.

Under BOP v0.10's retained product policy, the independent opening member cannot be appointed to evaluate the same tender.

The committee has 3–5 appointed members, with recorded designation, department and capacity; the secretary is recorded separately unless also validly appointed as a member. Each member personally chooses **No conflict to declare** or **Declare a conflict**, and accepts **I will keep bid information confidential and use it only for this evaluation.** A declared conflict stops that person's bid access immediately and creates the Accounting Officer's task. Ordinary unresolved declaration tasks do not stop already eligible members from reading or checking; collective decisions and final report signing require a complete eligible roster.

**Department and appointment reference (v0.6).** *Department.* A member's department is not typed. When the Accounting Officer selects a person on an appointment or replacement form, the system shows that person's department as read-only text beside the person and records it with the appointment. The department is the person's **recorded organisational affiliation**: the Organisation Unit recorded against that staff account for this purpose (Owner decision D1, §12.5). It is never taken from the tender's department, from a responsibility assignment held for another purpose, or from anything the appointing user enters. The server derives it again when the action commits; a department supplied by the browser is not accepted. (v0.7) The recorded affiliation is the person's **home organisation unit** (AUTH-ADR-001 v1.12 §4.8), read through `GetStaffHomeOrganisationUnit` (CFG-CHG-002 v0.19 §7.2); the department is that unit's name at the moment of the read. A person has one home unit or none, so the department is never ambiguous and never typed. Where none is recorded the form shows **Not recorded** as the department with the notice **Department not recorded. Ask your KenTender administrator to record it.** The appointment is **not** blocked: department plays no part in eligibility, which stays as defined in this section. The server reads the department again when the action commits and records it on the appointment as a **snapshot**; it stays in the roster history, and a later transfer, rename or clearing of the home unit never rewrites it. **Completing a blank.** Where a member's department was recorded as Not recorded and a home unit is later recorded for that person, the system completes that member's department once, on the current appointment only, and appends one entry to Appointment history with the unit, the administrator who recorded it and the time. It acts on the published `StaffHomeUnitChanged` event (CFG-CHG-002 v0.19 §4.10A); a replay does nothing further. A department that has been recorded is never replaced, and a former member's record is never completed. No task is created for the administrator, because Administrator and System Manager hold no My Work items (KT-STD-001 §3A.6); the administrator's list is the **Not recorded** filter of AUTH-DES-10 (AUTH-ADR-001 v1.12 §13.12). (v0.6 read: “When the affiliation is not a single clear value, free text is not the fallback: (a) where more than one affiliation is recorded, the Accounting Officer chooses one of that person's recorded affiliations; (b) where none is recorded, the person cannot be appointed until the staff data is corrected, and the form shows **{name} has no recorded department.** beside the person (an `EVL_MEMBER_INELIGIBLE` reason, §8). The department recorded at appointment stays in the roster history; a later change to staff data does not rewrite it. (a) and (b) are proposed handling, subject to Owner decision D2.” Withdrawn and superseded by this paragraph.)

*Appointment reference.* Nobody enters an appointment reference. The system generates it when the appointment action commits: **Appoint committee** and **Replace member** by the Accounting Officer, and **Assign secretary** by the Head of Procurement (Owner decision D3 asks the Project Owner to confirm the last two). The record stores the reference with the appointing officer, the trusted time of the action and the roster the action produced. The reference then appears in the appointment record and in **Appointment history**. It is unique, is never reused, cannot be edited, and a replayed command returns the original reference (§7.2). A replacement and a secretary assignment each receive their own reference. A rejected or failed action produces no appointment and no visible reference. The reference identifies that recorded appointment; whether the entity also issues a separate appointment letter or memo is outside this document. The form of the reference follows the examples in §9.1 (proposed generation rule, Owner decision D3). (v0.7) The form is the pattern the §9 examples show; the strings themselves are illustrative, and production numbering is not constrained to reproduce any pictured string. The reference is allocated inside the appointment's own transaction, so a failed action leaves no gap in the numbering. Seeds make numbering deterministic where a consumer needs it, and tests and screenshots follow the agreed numbering behaviour, not the pictured strings. Existing references are preserved as history.

**Roster terminology.** The **current roster** means the members whose appointments have not been replaced or ended. An **eligible member** is a current member with the required declaration and confidentiality acceptance, no unresolved conflict and no recorded inability to serve. The **current eligible roster** is complete only when every current appointee meets those conditions and the appointment has 3–5 members. Collective decisions require that complete roster to be personally present; report signing requires every member of that same roster, asynchronously. Former members never count. An unavailable or conflicted member is not silently removed to reduce participation.

Adviser appointment and adviser access are deferred from MVP 1. Evidence or customer confirmation obtained during due diligence is recorded by the appointed committee participants; supplying information grants no system access.

Replacement is a reasoned Accounting Officer appointment, not a chair shortcut. Preserve former membership, findings and participation. The incoming member reviews the full current record and signs only as themselves. A roster change before report delivery withdraws that report version from signing and requires a new version signed by the current roster. After delivery, retain the delivered report and use the correction process in §5.6.

## 4. Automated checks and human judgment

### 4.1 One fixed set of rules

Consume the published bid definition, exact response identities and mappings. Keep the four template groups: `EVG-ELIGIBILITY`, `EVG-TECHNICAL-COMPLIANCE`, `EVG-FINANCIAL`, `EVG-AWARD`. The last supplies the recommendation for downstream Award; it is not a fourth scoring screen or award authority. Administrative contacts and document acknowledgements remain **Not evaluated** as the template specifies.

Each evaluated requirement has its published text/reference, applicability, submitted value/evidence, permitted comparison and a result with a reason. Supported automatic comparisons are presence, a declared boolean or permitted choice, numeric minimum/maximum, date condition and published calculation. Units, rounding, inclusive boundaries and the relevant date come from the rule, not an evaluator's settings. Explicit published alternatives are respected; “or equivalent” needs review unless equivalence is already defined by an exact published rule. Free text is not automatically interpreted as compliance.

The curated template implementation must supply this executable meaning for each automatic rule. This extends the existing mappings; it does not add a rule builder. Before use, reconcile every executable check with the human-readable tender. No automatic default may fill a missing rule. A missing or conflicting rule creates a named review issue, keeps unaffected checks available and cannot support a positive recommendation until resolved against the unchanged issued tender. A code correction preserves the published rule and is rerun for every affected bid; a changed criterion requires the owning procurement process, not an Evaluation patch.

### 4.2 Clear results

| Result | Meaning and treatment |
|---|---|
| **Meets** | The applicable check is satisfied by its stated basis. Show whether this is a comparison of the offered response or a completed evidence check. |
| **Does not meet** | The available evidence establishes failure of the published requirement. Keep the exact reason for committee review. It is not a supplier rejection notification. |
| **Needs review** | Judgment, conflicting evidence or unavailable information prevents a supported answer. Never silently convert it to pass or fail. |
| **Not applicable** | Only when the published rule's applicability condition excludes this check; retain the reason. |

For requirements with mandatory evidence assessment, an automatic response comparison may read **Meets**, while the combined requirement remains **Needs review** until that evidence assessment is resolved. A presence check never verifies authenticity. Do not count both component checks as separate technical criteria.

Group result: any applicable mandatory failure yields **Does not meet**; otherwise any unresolved applicable requirement yields **Needs review**; otherwise **Meets**. Show unresolved issues even if another check has failed. A bid is **Responsive** only when all required eligibility and technical checks are satisfied; **Not responsive** when a supported mandatory failure exists; otherwise **Needs review**. These conclusions enter the committee report for review and signature. Later stages for an excluded bid show **Not assessed — mandatory requirement not met**, not invented zero values.

### 4.3 Where people act

Members add a finding against a specific requirement, with a result, reason and evidence reference. A system finding cannot be overwritten. Challenges and contrary findings create a discussion item. The committee can resolve evidence interpretation under the published rule; it cannot waive a failed mandatory condition, change a threshold, adjust a price or select a preferred bidder. A suspected calculation or rule defect goes to the technical issue route and is recalculated consistently after correction.

Straightforward evidence checks may be recorded by an appointed member and reviewed by everyone in the report. No second approval is required for each check. Collective discussion is required for challenged findings, material interpretations, clarification decisions, due-diligence outcomes affecting recommendation and an unresolved recommendation. Members may raise concerns at any time before delivery. Report signing remains each member's own review of the complete conclusion. No independent “Accept all results” or per-check member-approval command exists.

Saving a finding as **Needs review**, raising a concern, or recording a contrary finding creates or updates one chair discussion item for the same bid/requirement. Routine evidence checks with a clear supported result create no extra approval. Each collective resolution links the concern, evidence, result and reasons. The chair or secretary uses **Record conclusion** for a resolved finding or explicit qualified-report disposition; other routes use their specialised actions below. A resolution that remains Needs review must state the next action: clarification, verification, technical repair or an explicit qualified report. For clarification or verification, open the existing question or verification-plan form and commit its specialised command as the conclusion; do not require a preceding Record conclusion click. For technical repair, the existing issue action records the concern and its support reference. Record conclusion handles resolved findings and explicit qualified-report outcomes. The discussion item clears only when its attributed resolved/qualified conclusion, specialised clarification authorisation or verification plan, or durable linked support issue is committed. Clarification creates the secretary’s send task; verification creates participant/lead tasks; technical routing creates the named support task; qualification remains visible in the report. Opening a form or reading does not clear work. Prior findings and each member’s disagreement remain visible.

### 4.4 Financial comparison and recommendation

Use exact decimal KES amounts. Preserve original price lines, taxes, discounts and the submitted/read-out total. The comparison displays **Submitted total**, each expressly permitted **Evaluation adjustment**, and **Evaluated total** separately. Zero adjustments are shown as **None**. Never write a computed amount back into the bid. Apply the published financial rule; do not introduce a preference margin, tax treatment, discount or exchange rate after closing.

Rank only responsive bids whose required financial checks are resolved, lowest evaluated total first. Pending bids remain visible outside a **Provisional comparison**; no final recommendation is produced while their resolution could change the outcome. Equal evaluated totals receive the same rank. Use a published lawful tie-break only if present; otherwise report **No single recommendation — equal evaluated totals**, retaining the tied bids for the Head of Procurement's next lawful action. No random selection or new tie-break occurs here.

**Comparison labels.** An unresolved prerequisite shows **Needs review** in Evaluated total and **Not ranked** in Position. Not ranked means the bid has no numerical position because responsiveness or a required financial check is unresolved or the bid is not responsive. For a nonresponsive bid, subsequent financial assessment shows **Not assessed — mandatory requirement not met**; its submitted total remains visible as a source fact, never an evaluated or recommended amount. **Provisional comparison** labels the comparison as a whole while unresolved bids could affect the outcome; it is not a bid result or lifecycle state.

Arithmetic inconsistencies remain visible. Where the issued tender supplies a lawful disposition, apply and explain it without changing the submitted sum. Without one, record the unresolved discrepancy and deliver an appropriately qualified report with no unsupported recommendation. A committee explanation cannot substitute for a missing legal or published basis.

Funding is separate: an authoritative budget comparison can report a shortfall, but cannot make a responsive bid fail, alter ranking or cause automatic cancellation. The report names the highest-ranked responsive bid and the funding issue. It does not authorise expenditure. If no bid is responsive, generate **No responsive bids** with every reason. A single responsive bid can be recommended; there is no minimum competition count invented by this module.

## 5. Work, discussion and completion

### 5.1 Receive the opened bids

**Scope gate.** Read the authoritative published tender definition and require all of: Open Tender; IT Goods with published `product_key` = `IT-EQUIPMENT-OPEN-V1`; one lot; KES; fixed price; lowest-evaluated-responsive method. Never infer these from a title or use defaults. A known unsupported scope creates no Evaluation case or tasks. Missing or conflicting required scope metadata creates a named issue for the Tenders owner, without creating evaluation preparation or assessment tasks; retry the same publication/source identity after the owner repairs the binding. The proposed producer contract in §6 must bind these meanings to its actual published fields; this document does not claim that binding already exists. Identifier provenance: TPR-CHG-001 v0.13, Tender field contract, defines `product_key` as `IT-EQUIPMENT-OPEN-V1`; STD-TPL-001 v0.10 identifies the same value as `template_key`. Its separate `product_profile_id` is `GOODS-IT-SIMPLE-V1`; do not substitute that profile identifier for the product/template key.

**Preparation trigger.** On the first authoritative publication of an in-scope tender, Evaluation automatically records one preparation case and creates the Accounting Officer’s appointment task and the Head of Procurement’s secretary task. Publication supplies only the published tender reference and scope, never sealed-box facts. This is an Evaluation consumer operation, `EnsureEvaluationPreparation`, with no new publication approval or user start action. A replay or addendum does not duplicate the case, appointments or tasks. On valid nonempty opening intake, the same operation creates any missing preparation/tasks before attaching the source. If publication notification was missed, intake is a recovery path, not permission to skip appointment. A final no-bids or cancellation outcome takes precedence over delayed publication events and prevents new preparation tasks.

Accept one verified nonempty BOP completion, with unchanged packages, the issued definition/mappings, register, minutes, opening exceptions and exact source versions. Record receipt automatically; the separately appointed committee's access is resolved by Evaluation. BOP's “takes up” requirement is satisfied by this recorded owner intake, not a manual receipt approval. No assessment intake from an incomplete or cancelled opening. Appointment may exist before intake. A verified final BOP outcome of no bids closes any existing preparation as **No evaluation required**, preserving appointments, declarations and history. Clear outstanding preparation tasks and record the source outcome once. If no preparation exists, retain the BOP outcome without creating an evaluation case. Create no assessment, report or signing tasks. An incomplete opening or failed intake is never treated as an empty opening.

Automatic checks start once a valid source package is available. Committee appointment governs human access, not permission for a backend comparison. A duplicate completion produces no duplicate case, check run or task. A failed intake automatically records a source issue for the named support owner and a safe waiting notice for the AO/Head if no secretary exists; the appointed secretary later receives its recovery view. Retry preserves the same operation identity; never display it as “no bids”.

### 5.2 Discuss and record the work

Individual work is asynchronous. For a collective decision, **Start discussion** creates the session and records the chair’s own attendance in the same action. Other current members join personally. All members of the current eligible roster must remain present when a conclusion is recorded. Secretary or chair records the subject, affected requirement/bid, evidence considered, conclusion and reasons. Actual attendance and times are retained. For in-person and remote discussion alike, starting, joining and leaving record the actor’s own participation. If current participation cannot be established, the member is unavailable for collective decisions until they personally rejoin. The participation mechanism and its timing belong to the technical implementation, not a fixed business timeout. This confirms authenticated participation, not physical attendance inferred from a camera. Presence is rechecked on each collective command. Loss of a member pauses collective decisions; reading and individual notes continue. Resumption requires the complete current eligible roster. The secretary cannot manufacture attendance.

Members write their own disagreement and reasons; the recorder cannot attribute unconfirmed words to them. A signature confirms that the report is the member's reviewed record, including any disagreement; it is not a claim of unanimity. No automatic averaging, majority rule or casting vote. If members cannot establish one supported recommendation, record the competing positions and **No agreed recommendation** for downstream resolution. Disagreement itself is not a reason to suppress a report or replace a member.

Proceedings records only material discussion, findings and conclusions. No verbatim transcript, compulsory audio/video, duplicate minutes or additional meeting approval is required. Members can meet physically or use an external call; the product records participation and outcomes. One evaluation may have several sessions.

### 5.3 Written clarification

The committee records the exact question, affected published requirement and original response, permissible reply scope and reply deadline. Set a future deadline that allows a reasonable response period within the operative tender/evaluation limits; the chair sees any date conflict before authorisation. No automatic 24- or 48-hour period is imposed. The chair authorises it during the recorded collective discussion; the secretary sends that unchanged request. Authorisation records the committee’s question and decision in one operation; no preceding duplicate conclusion-entry action is required. No separate procurement approval. The supplier sees ordinary instructions and the deadline in EAT.

The authenticated supplier submits one response with allowed supporting explanation/evidence. The immutable original bid remains intact. A draft reply can be edited until sent. A sent response cannot be replaced; further explanation requires a new linked request. Supporting files are restricted to the question and shared upload security limits; they cannot introduce a new offer or cure a material omission through a hidden resubmission.

Timeliness uses the trusted received instant: at or before the request deadline is on time. While the request remains open, later replies are recorded and labelled **Received late** for committee disposition; they are not automatically accepted or discarded. No reply produces **Reply overdue**, not automatic rejection. The committee records the final disposition under the original rule. That action closes the request atomically, including where no reply was received. Withdrawal or authoritative tender cancellation also closes it. Deadline expiry alone does not close it. A submitted reply already consumes the one-reply allowance. After closure, retain the question, reply or unsent draft, disposition and trusted closure time; show **This clarification is closed** and allow no further draft writes or replies. A concurrent reply is accepted only if committed before closure and must be included in the disposition; otherwise reject it without changing evidence or findings. Any further question requires a new linked request. An attempted price/specification change is retained as correspondence but excluded from the bid and comparison.

The request is available in the supplier workspace even if its email notice fails. Record **Delivery problem** truthfully; retry sends the same notice and preserves the request identity. A corrected question requires withdrawal with a reason and a new linked request/deadline; an unsent draft can simply be edited. The old request and any reply remain readable. Unaffected evaluation continues while waiting.

### 5.4 Due diligence

After comparison, undertake due diligence where required by the issued tender or decided under the applicable legal basis. Record scope, evidence, actual participants, findings and effect on recommendation. Do not add qualification criteria. If not undertaken, record the basis in the report through the existing committee conclusion; do not make a separate clearance click mandatory.

For MVP 1, due diligence is carried out by named eligible members of the current evaluation committee. During a collective discussion, the chair uses **Record verification plan** to record the committee’s scope, basis, selected participants and one participant as lead in a single operation. This is work allocation within the appointed committee, not a new statutory appointment or approval. The lead coordinates the record; each participant records their own observations and signs their own targets. A scope or participant change uses the same command with a reason and retained history; it supersedes any frozen verification report and recreates the required tasks and proofs. No adviser or external user is appointed through this action. Findings that affect the recommendation return to collective discussion after all required verification proofs are recorded.

A negative finding affecting recommendation requires a recorded committee conclusion. Only after that conclusion may the system propose the next eligible ranked bidder and identify any required checks for that bidder. It never silently substitutes the runner-up. A separate due-diligence report carries its actual participants' page-initial and final-report-signature targets; Evaluation supplies every rendered page for initials and the final report for signature, with participant name/designation, to the shared signing interface. This is not replaced by a secretary's generic confirmation.

### 5.5 Report and delivery

The report is generated continuously from the authoritative record. It includes tender/criteria versions, appointment and changes, the opened bid inventory, per-bid eligibility and technical results/reasons, financial comparison, clarifications and their dispositions, material discussion and disagreement, due diligence or its stated basis, recommendation/outcome and qualifications, and member signature details. Supporting evidence is linked and exportable. The secretary edits only narrative that is not an authoritative source fact.

**Send for signing** freezes the report and all material annexes as one exact version, once every required result is either resolved or explicitly included in a committee-recorded qualified outcome. Every sent clarification must have a final disposition or recorded withdrawal before freezing; unanswered requests require an explicit no-reply disposition. Ordinary unanswered questions cannot be concealed as “complete”. No meeting may remain active. A positive recommendation requires any undertaken verification exercise to have its complete findings, required participant proofs and committee disposition. An incomplete exercise must be explicitly recorded in a qualified report with no unsupported recommendation. The report must distinguish an eligible recommendation from no responsive bids, an unresolved tie, an unresolved rule/discrepancy, no agreed recommendation or a funding concern. A qualified report is a completed account of the issue, not a successful award recommendation.

Every member of the current eligible roster personally reviews and signs that version. A member can instead **Raise a concern**, returning the report to review with their reason. Dissent can remain in an accurately recorded report. **Revise report** also returns it to review with a reason. Either action ends signature collection for that version; old signatures remain historical. A new frozen version requires all fresh signatures. A refusal or inability to sign creates the chair's follow-up task; it does not count as consent. If service is impossible, the Accounting Officer's reasoned appointment process applies, without signing for the departed member.

Before freezing and again before delivery, recheck current source impact, roster, cancellation/suspension and tender validity. A newly material source/roster change returns the case to Reviewing with the reason and secretary task; preserve collected proofs against the superseded target. Validity expiry prevents an unsupported positive recommendation. If the frozen report still makes that recommendation or omits the expiry, return it to Reviewing for a new version and fresh signatures. A report that explicitly records the expiry and **No current recommendation — tender validity expired** may be frozen, signed and delivered, subject to the other guards. Expiry alone does not repeatedly invalidate that qualified report. A suspension alone preserves the signing state but pauses completion.

The final required valid signature commits **Report sent** and one durable delivery event for the Head of Procurement. No chair “complete”, “activate”, “release” or recipient receipt approval. Email failure does not reverse an in-product delivery. A delivery-system failure shows a retryable issue while preserving signatures; report status becomes **Report sent** only when the recipient record and task exist.

### 5.6 Return, correction and later evidence

Before a downstream decision, the Head of Procurement may **Return for correction** with a specific reason. Preserve the delivered report; reopen the evaluation as **Reviewing** and prepare a new numbered report with a change summary and fresh signatures. The Head of Procurement cannot directly change results or ranking. Committee-initiated corrections follow the same versioned route and notify the recipient.

After a downstream award decision, do not reopen or overwrite the basis of that decision. Record a linked correction notice and notify the Head of Procurement for the owning review process. Downstream decision status must be authoritative; unavailable status cannot be assumed to mean “no award”. The original report and notice stay accessible. The Head receives **Review report correction for {tender}**. Each supplement or correction task is keyed by evaluation, immutable source event and recipient responsibility; its recorded review/return/correction must identify that source event. A replay creates no duplicate. Concurrent supplements and corrections remain separate, and clearing one never clears another. Downstream review remains owned by the receiving process, not a new Evaluation approval.

BOP supplements are appended as new source evidence. Before delivery, a member must record whether they affect any finding; material changes require recalculation and, if already signing, a new report. Before delivery, route **Review opening update for {tender}** to the chair; any eligible appointed member may record the impact and clear that shared item. After delivery, route **Review opening update for {tender}** to the chair and **Review opening update for {tender}** to the Head of Procurement. The chair’s impact record clears the chair’s item; the Head’s item clears only on their recorded review or return/correction action, never on the chair’s assessment alone. Do not mutate the completed report, replay opening completion or automatically replace the recommendation.

### 5.7 Timers, suspension and cancellation

Display the statutory evaluation deadline and tender-validity end from the authoritative dated rules. The legal computation, source and timezone must be inspectable; do not guess a validity expiry from “120 days” without the governing counting rule. Overdue evaluation is highlighted to the chair and Head of Procurement, or the AO and Head of Procurement if there is no chair; work and truthful reporting remain possible. Expiry prevents a fresh positive award recommendation unless the tender owner supplies a lawful, timely extension record. Evaluation cannot extend validity or ask suppliers to revive an expired tender through clarification.

An authoritative suspension pauses new committee decisions, requests and report signing. Appointment and personal-declaration actions are available during suspension only where the recorded authoritative instruction explicitly permits those administrative actions; absence of permission keeps them paused. The owner event records that scope, and permitted actions retain their ordinary authority and access restrictions; preserve drafts, existing evidence and history. Resumption comes only from the same owning authority; recheck source, roster and validity before allowing further decisions. Existing proofs remain historical evidence and satisfy the current report only if its targets and roster are unchanged. Incoming correspondence is retained during the pause without changing findings. Authoritative cancellation closes the evaluation and supplier reply actions, withdraws pending signing tasks and preserves a factual partial record. It creates no normal evaluation completion or award recommendation. An already delivered report remains historical and is marked with the later cancellation. Evaluation does not add its own cancel or resume approval.
