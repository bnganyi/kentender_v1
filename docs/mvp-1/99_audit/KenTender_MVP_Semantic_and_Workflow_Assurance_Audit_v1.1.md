# KenTender MVP Semantic and Workflow Assurance Audit

**Document ID:** KENTENDER-MVP-SWA-1.1  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Date:** 11 August 2026  
**Status:** Draft for product-owner validation — not approved for implementation  
**Supersedes:** KENTENDER-MVP-SWA-1.0  
**Scope:** Strategy Alignment, Budget & Funding, Demands, Procurement Planning, organisation scope, authorisation surfaces and canonical demo data

## 1. Purpose and control decision

This audit establishes a controlled recovery baseline for work completed from Strategy through Procurement Planning. It does not require the platform to be restarted. It separates sound foundations from unsupported fields, roles and workflow stages.

The governing rule is:

> KenTender shall omit a feature, field, record or workflow stage unless its real-world task, accountable actor, source, state consequence and downstream consumer can all be stated plainly.

Existing requirements, Stitch packs and Cursor packs remain evidence of prior work, but are provisional until reconciled against this audit and the Cross-Module Operating Model.

No document may mark itself `Approved`, `Locked` or an implementation baseline without explicit recorded product-owner approval.

## 2. Correction to version 1.0

Version 1.0 incorrectly introduced a routine second Head-of-Department or Organisation-Unit sign-off after the Procurement Planner had prepared the Plan Item. That requirement is withdrawn.

The normal operating flow is:

1. the Requester creates the Demand;
2. the responsible Head of Department approves or returns the Demand;
3. the Procurement Planner converts the Approved Demand into a Plan Item and completes the procurement-planning decisions;
4. the Budget Officer or authorised Finance role confirms funding;
5. the Head of Procurement completes the applicable professional review or approval;
6. an eligible Plan Item proceeds to Tender preparation.

The HoD's Demand approval is the normal evidence that the requirement is legitimate, owned by the OU and authorised for planning. Planning shall not request the same approval again merely because Procurement has added method, schedule, aggregation or lotting decisions.

HoD reapproval is required only where Planning makes a material business change to what was approved, such as:

- changing the owning OU;
- materially changing scope, quantity, delivery requirement or intended outcome;
- materially increasing the estimated value;
- combining Demands in a way that changes OU accountability; or
- otherwise changing a fact for which the HoD is the accountable decision-maker.

A configuration may require annual batch certification of the OU-scoped departmental plan where formal evidence is necessary. That is a single certification over the annual item set, not a separate contribution workflow and not routine per-item reapproval.

## 3. Evidence and interpretation boundary

The Kenyan legal baseline includes:

- section 53 of the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, addressing realistic annual procurement planning, approved budgets, sufficient funds, preference and reservation requirements, approval and publication; and
- regulations 40–42 of the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Regulations, addressing departmental and consolidated annual procurement planning and prescribed plan content.

Primary sources:

- [Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, section 53](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2015/33/eng%402022-12-31)
- [Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Regulations, regulations 40–43 and 71](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2020/69/eng%402020-12-24)

These provisions do not, by themselves, justify a duplicate KenTender contribution editor, a second item-authorship process or a universal item-level HoD reapproval. Any legal interpretation that creates an additional workflow stage must identify the exact evidence required and why an existing approval or generated projection is insufficient.

## 4. Product truths retained

1. KenTender supports any public Procuring Entity and does not assume a Ministry hierarchy.
2. Every owned record has one `procuring_entity` and, where applicable, one `owner_org_unit`.
3. One authorised creation scope is shown explicitly; multiple eligible scopes require deliberate selection; no eligible scope blocks creation. No first-row, workspace-filter or Administrator fallback is allowed.
4. Technical and business references are generated by the system. Users do not maintain codes.
5. The lifecycle spine is:

   **Strategy → Budget & Funding → Demand → Procurement Plan Item → Tender → Contract**

6. Upstream facts are inherited downstream and are not re-entered or silently changed.
7. Strategy Value Commitments are defined in Strategy and inherited as governed context. Downstream modules add only concrete decisions they own.
8. The Plan Item is the operational execution unit. The Plan Version is the approval and immutability boundary.
9. Adding an Approved Demand to an Approved Plan quietly creates or reuses one Draft successor. The current Approved version remains operational until the successor is approved.
10. Unauthorised task forms and task actions are absent rather than merely disabled. Record visibility is separate from task authority.
11. The financial system remains authoritative for complete budgeting and expenditure. KenTender retains the procurement funding controls required to progress the procurement lifecycle.

## 5. Mandatory concept-admission gate

Every proposed entity, field, status, action, screen, tab, service or notification must answer all of the following:

| Gate | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Real-world task | What recognisable business task is completed? |
| Accountable actor | Who owns the decision or fact? |
| Source | Is the value entered, selected, inherited, calculated or integrated? |
| State consequence | What record or state changes? |
| Downstream consumer | Which later task or control uses it? |
| Legal/business basis | Why must KenTender retain it? |
| Omission consequence | What fails if it is absent? |
| UI necessity | Why must it be visible or editable here? |
| Testability | What observable test proves it? |

If any answer is missing or circular, the concept is excluded from MVP 1. “For completeness,” “for audit,” “for reporting” and “future use” are insufficient without a named consumer and consequence.

## 6. Corrected cross-module actor and decision model

| Stage | Accountable task | Primary actor | Output | Explicit exclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Approve Strategy outcomes, targets and commitments | Configured Strategy authority | Versioned Strategy references and Strategy Value Commitments | No downstream treatment questionnaire |
| Budget & Funding | Register procurement funding and control availability | Budget Officer, configured reviewer/authority | Active Budget Lines and auditable funding controls | No duplicate financial-system budgeting function |
| Demand creation | Describe the business need | Requester | Draft Demand | No Strategy, Budget or method selection |
| Demand approval | Confirm legitimacy, ownership, scope and priority | Head of Department / Business Approver | Approved Demand | No procurement method or Plan Item authoring |
| Planning | Form and complete the executable Plan Item | Procurement Planner | Draft Plan Item in the applicable Plan Version | No routine second HoD sign-off |
| Funding confirmation | Confirm the proposed funding assignment and availability | Budget Officer / authorised Finance role | Funding sign-off tied to the planned requirement | Cannot be skipped because matching was automatic |
| Procurement review | Confirm professional planning completeness | Head of Procurement / configured professional authority | Reviewed or approved planning decision | Does not repeat HoD business approval |
| Tender preparation | Create the Tender from an eligible Plan Item | Authorised Procurement role | Tender linked to immutable handoff evidence | No unplanned or unauthorised source |

Where a Procuring Entity requires another statutory plan authority, it is configuration over the Plan Version approval route; it is not a reason to invent another item-authoring workflow.

## 7. Cross-module disposition

### 7.1 Keep

| Concept | Reason |
|---|---|
| Generic PE/OU scope and explicit scope selection | Required for national applicability and correct ownership |
| Versioned approved records | Preserves immutable legal and operational baselines |
| Generated references | Retains traceability without manual codes |
| Strategy Value Commitment | Required Strategy-to-procurement linkage when simplified and inherited |
| Budget, Budget Line, Reservation and Commitment | Minimum procurement funding-control baseline |
| Mandatory Budget Officer sign-off | Required even when the system recommends or assigns funding automatically |
| Demand, Need Item and HoD approval | Establishes authorised business need |
| Plan, Plan Version, Plan Item, Plan Item Version and Demand Allocation | Correct planning boundaries |
| Approved-plan Draft successor | Supports common additions without interrupting the current plan |
| Neutral record surface and authorised task surface | Prevents unauthorised workflow-form exposure |
| Canonical deterministic seed story | Supports repeatable development and demonstration |

### 7.2 Correct

| Current concept | Required correction |
|---|---|
| User-maintained Strategy and hierarchy codes | Generate stable references; display meaningful titles and paths |
| Overlapping Active strategic plans | Control by plan type, ownership scope and effective period, not merely by code |
| `Plan Value Commitment` and inconsistent labels | Use **Strategy Value Commitment** consistently |
| Forced one-primary-target Budget classification | Permit only Strategy links supported by the approved budget structure |
| Demand aggregation decision | Demand may flag similarity; Planning owns the actual package-formation decision |
| Planning aggregation control shown by default | One selected Demand creates one Plan Item; show aggregation only when another compatible Demand is intentionally added to that Draft Plan Item |
| Approved-plan addition | Open or reuse one Draft successor quietly; do not present plan versioning as a separate operational task |
| Planning-stage HoD sign-off | Remove from the normal path; use targeted reapproval only for a material business change |
| Finance position | Retain mandatory Budget Officer sign-off after the planning proposal is available and before procurement approval |
| Approval-form visibility | Task forms are accessible only to authorised actors for a live task; other viewers use neutral detail surfaces |

### 7.3 Remove from MVP 1

- generic Budget Value Treatment or funding-treatment questionnaires;
- generic Demand Value Treatment tables;
- generic Planning statutory/Strategy treatment fields and unsupported planned-treatment values;
- `Departmental Submission`, Organisation Unit contribution editors and contributor roles;
- universal `OU_SIGNOFF` decisions for Plan Items;
- default aggregation decisions for a single selected Demand;
- blank or zero-filled inapplicable rationale/compliance controls;
- Administrator operational-scope fallbacks; and
- dashboards or management workflows with no validated operational consumer.

Removing generic treatment records does not remove Strategy Value Commitments or applicable statutory controls. Those remain inherited context or concrete, named decisions at the point where they have an operational consequence.

### 7.4 Defer

- a reusable Public Value Objective applicability and enforcement engine;
- advanced Strategy corrective-action workflows;
- funding and Demand performance dashboards;
- manual or simulated expenditure operations outside labelled test fixtures;
- automated aggregation benefit recommendations;
- advanced publication and open-contracting behaviour beyond a clean handoff contract; and
- annual departmental-plan certification unless its exact evidence and actor journey are validated.

## 8. Module correction priorities

### 8.1 Strategy Alignment

Preserve versioned plans, targets, measurements and Strategy Value Commitments. Remove manual codes and generic downstream treatment instructions. Resolve Active-plan overlap by business type and scope. Reassess the Public Value Objective rules engine before admitting it to MVP 1.

### 8.2 Budget & Funding

Preserve direct Budget registration, Budget Lines, activation, availability calculations, reservations, commitments and revisions. Remove generic funding-treatment records. Keep complete financial budgeting and expenditure in the financial system. Strategy links are inherited or selected only where the approved budget structure supports them.

### 8.3 Demands

Preserve simple Requester capture, explicit PE/OU ownership, HoD approval and inherited context. The Requester does not select Strategy, Budget, method or planning data. Remove generic value-treatment and package-formation decisions. Align the location of mandatory Budget Officer sign-off with the corrected actor flow.

### 8.4 Procurement Planning

The corrected journey is:

1. the planner opens the annual Plan or its current Draft successor;
2. **Add Plan Item** opens the eligible Approved Demand selector;
3. selecting one Demand creates one Draft Plan Item;
4. the Plan Item editor completes description, method, arrangement, schedule and indicative lotting;
5. Finance confirms funding;
6. the Head of Procurement or configured professional authority reviews or approves the plan;
7. an eligible Plan Item proceeds to Tender preparation.

For an Approved Plan, steps 2–4 occur in one quietly created or reused Draft successor. The current Approved version remains operational.

The Plan Item editor does not select the Demand again and does not show a default aggregation question. Aggregation appears only when the planner explicitly adds another compatible Approved Demand to the same Draft Plan Item. A separate-package decision must create actual separate Plan Items; it cannot be a stored radio value without operational effect.

Remove the routine OU contribution/sign-off screens and services. If Planning materially changes a HoD-owned fact, route that changed fact for targeted HoD reapproval; do not reopen the entire planning workflow.

## 9. Organisation scope and authorisation

The following are mandatory across all four modules:

- list results, counts, queues, actions and direct routes use the same server-side scope rules;
- users with multiple eligible PEs/OUs select deliberately;
- Administrator status alone provides no operational authority, but an administrator may receive explicit operational scope like any other user;
- record-view authority does not imply workflow-task authority;
- unauthorised task routes return a controlled access result and do not render the form; and
- task notifications and work queues are capability-scoped.

## 10. Canonical demo-data correction

Preserve the coherent story and arithmetic:

- Ministry of Health principal Budget Line: KES 480,000,000;
- principal Approved Demand and Active Plan Item: KES 455,000,000;
- contract commitment: KES 310,000,000;
- remaining reservation: KES 145,000,000;
- returned Demand corrected from KES 95,000,000 to KES 80,000,000;
- Draft Revision 2 consolidated Plan value: KES 535,000,000;
- Approved Version 1 remains operational while Draft Revision 2 is prepared; and
- County Government of Kisumu remains the minimal second-entity isolation story.

The next canonical version shall remove generic treatment records, contribution records and routine planning-stage HoD sign-offs. It shall retain the original HoD Demand approval, mandatory Finance sign-off, Strategy lineage and deterministic identities.

## 11. Document and implementation recovery sequence

1. Accept or amend the Cross-Module Operating Model.
2. Run a read-only implementation inventory and classify every affected artifact as Keep, Correct, Remove or Defer.
3. Reissue requirements in dependency order: Strategy, Budget & Funding, Demands, Procurement Planning.
4. Reissue the canonical demo-data contract against the accepted semantics.
5. Regenerate Stitch prompts from accepted requirements.
6. Regenerate Cursor packs from accepted requirements and Stitch screen contracts.
7. Implement corrections in small reversible change sets with migration or seed-rebuild decisions stated explicitly.
8. Pass module and cross-module acceptance tests before resuming feature expansion.

Older files are retained as history and marked `Superseded`, `Withdrawn` or `Provisional`; they are not silently overwritten.

## 12. Acceptance tests for the corrected baseline

The baseline is not complete until tests prove:

- explicit PE/OU selection and isolation;
- no Administrator or first-assignment fallback;
- no user-maintained technical codes;
- Requester capture contains only facts the Requester can reasonably know;
- HoD approval occurs once in the normal path;
- mandatory Finance sign-off cannot be bypassed;
- the planner authors the Plan Item;
- no second HoD approval occurs unless a material HoD-owned fact changes;
- an Approved Plan remains operational during a Draft revision;
- one selected Demand creates one Plan Item without a false aggregation decision;
- unauthorised workflow forms are inaccessible, not merely disabled;
- removed generic treatment and contribution concepts are absent from schema, services, UI, seeds and tests; and
- the canonical seed can be rerun deterministically with cross-entity isolation.

## 13. Status of existing artifacts

Until each artifact is reconciled, the following are not safe implementation authorities:

- Strategy Requirements, Stitch and Cursor packs;
- Budget & Funding Requirements, Stitch and Cursor packs;
- Demands Requirements, Stitch and Cursor packs;
- Procurement Planning Requirements, Stitch and Cursor packs; and
- Canonical Demo Data Contract 2.5.

The generic PE/OU scope model, immutable-version pattern, funding reservation model, Plan/Version/Item distinction and canonical story are substantively retained, subject to implementation verification.

## 14. Conclusion

The platform should not be discarded. The safest correction preserves working foundations and removes only concepts that fail the admission gate. The highest-risk errors are semantic: invented treatment fields, duplicated approval, contribution indirection, ambiguous scope and task-form exposure. Correcting those upstream, then regenerating design and implementation packs, is safer than patching individual screens or rewriting the platform.
