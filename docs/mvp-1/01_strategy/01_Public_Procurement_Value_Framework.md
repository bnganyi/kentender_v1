Public Procurement Value Framework

**1\. What the legal framework expects**

The governing principles can be organised into the following domains:

| **Domain**                                                                      | **Legal foundation**                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fairness, equity, transparency, competition and cost-effectiveness              | Constitution Article 227                                  |
| National values, inclusion, human dignity and protection of marginalised groups | Constitution Article 10                                   |
| Equality and freedom from discrimination                                        | Constitution Article 27                                   |
| Youth and marginalised-group affirmative action                                 | Constitution Articles 55 and 56                           |
| Integrity and professional conduct                                              | Leadership and Integrity Act and PPADA                    |
| Openness, accountability, public participation and prudent use of public money  | Constitution Article 201                                  |
| Efficient, effective and economic use of resources                              | Constitution Article 232                                  |
| Value for money                                                                 | PPADA section 3                                           |
| Local industry and citizen contractors                                          | PPADA section 3 and preference and reservation provisions |
| Sustainable development and environmental protection                            | PPADA section 3                                           |
| Responsible procurement, inventory, contract and disposal management            | PPADA lifecycle provisions                                |

The constitutional standards are not peripheral references. PPADA expressly makes them governing principles of procurement and disposal. [Constitution of Kenya](https://kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution?utm_source=chatgpt.com), [PPRA-PPADA editions](https://ppra.go.ke/ppda/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**2\. Areas that should receive the same treatment as ESG**

**A. Service effectiveness**

The system should ask whether a procurement achieved the public or operational outcome for which it was initiated.

Examples:

- Hospital equipment placed into service and functioning
- Roads completed and available for use
- ICT system adoption and availability
- Medicines delivered before stock-out
- School facilities operational before the academic term

This requires outcome indicators in Strategy Alignment and Demand Intake, followed by acceptance measures and contract KPIs. Completion of a procurement process is not the same as successful service delivery.

**B. Process efficiency**

Electronic procurement should materially reduce time and administrative effort.

The system can measure:

- Demand-to-approval time
- Planning-to-publication time
- Evaluation duration
- Number of approval returns
- Duplicate data requests
- Manual document generation
- Time spent resolving incomplete submissions
- Contract-to-payment cycle
- Disposal processing time

These measures support the constitutional expectation of efficient, effective and economic use of resources under Article 232. [Public Service (Values and Principles) Act](https://kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2015/1a/eng%402022-12-31?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The important design principle is that KenTender should capture timestamps and workflow events automatically. Users should not complete separate "efficiency reports."

**C. Economy and whole-life value**

The current approach should expand beyond budget availability and tender price.

The system should support:

- Market and historical price benchmarks
- Total cost of ownership
- Operating and maintenance costs
- Energy and consumable costs
- Financing implications
- Contract variations
- Disposal costs or proceeds
- Cost avoidance through repair, reuse or aggregation
- Framework and bulk-purchasing opportunities
- Planned versus realised savings

The National Procurement Policy expressly connects sustainable procurement and value for money to the whole lifecycle. [National Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Policy 2020](https://www.treasury.go.ke/sites/default/files/Policies/NATIONAL-PUBLIC-PROCUREMENT-AND-ASSET-DISPOSAL-POLICY-2020.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

"Lowest price" and "value for money" must therefore remain distinct concepts in KenTender.

**D. Demand optimisation**

One of the largest savings opportunities exists before a tender is created.

Demand Intake should determine:

- Is the requirement necessary?
- Is the item already available elsewhere in the entity?
- Can an existing asset be repaired, reassigned or upgraded?
- Can similar departmental demands be aggregated?
- Is the requested quantity supported by consumption data?
- Is the delivery schedule aligned to actual need?
- Is leasing, framework procurement or another model more economical?
- Which existing assets will be displaced or disposed of?

This is where the system can prevent unnecessary procurement rather than merely process it efficiently.

**E. Competition and market development**

The system should actively monitor whether procurement design promotes meaningful competition.

Possible indicators include:

- Number of responsive bidders
- Single-bid tenders
- Repeated awards to the same supplier
- Market concentration
- Tender requirements that unnecessarily restrict participation
- Excessive qualification thresholds
- Bidder withdrawals
- Lots that could improve SME participation
- Failed and repeatedly re-advertised tenders
- Procurement methods used and their justification

This directly supports fairness, competition and cost-effectiveness under Article 227.

The system should flag risks for review, but it must not automatically infer wrongdoing from a low bidder count.

**F. Inclusion and economic development**

PPADA goes substantially beyond environmental sustainability. It explicitly addresses preferences, reservations, local industry and citizen contractors.

KenTender should therefore support:

- Applicable preference and reservation schemes
- Procurement budget allocation targets
- Opportunities reserved for eligible groups
- Local content or local industry objectives where legally applicable
- Participation versus actual award values
- Contract performance by beneficiary category
- Payment timeliness
- Subcontracting commitments
- Geographic or regional participation, where lawfully configured
- Skills and knowledge transfer

Section 158 requires preferences and reservations to be integrated into procurement plans and reported using disaggregated data. [PPADA, section 158](https://eacc.go.ke/default/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/PPDA.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

This cannot be handled only as a certificate check during bid submission. It must start in strategy, budgeting and planning and continue through contract delivery.

**G. Integrity and corruption prevention**

Legal declarations are necessary but insufficient. The system can reduce actual integrity risk through structural controls:

- Separation of duties
- Conflict-of-interest declarations linked to specific proceedings
- Restricted access before bid opening
- Immutable bid-submission records
- Controlled evaluation access
- Full decision and change history
- Detection of unusual variations
- Repeated direct procurement analysis
- Related-party and supplier identity checks
- Bid-pattern indicators
- Approval overrides with recorded reasons
- Supplier sanctions and debarment controls

These are not generic "anti-corruption scores." They are specific preventive, detective and evidentiary controls.

**H. Budget and fiscal discipline**

Budget compliance should be more than confirming that a budget line exists.

The system should connect:

- Strategic objective
- Approved budget
- Procurement plan
- Demand
- Tender estimate
- Award value
- Commitments
- Contract variations
- Payments
- Final cost
- Disposal proceeds

It should detect:

- Procurement without sufficient funding
- Fragmentation or splitting
- Commitments exceeding approval
- Repeated unplanned procurement
- Material estimate-to-award variance
- Unfunded contract variations
- Delayed payment exposure
- Procurements carried forward without justification

This operationalises Article 201's requirements for openness, accountability and prudent use of public money. [Constitution of Kenya, Article 201](https://kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2010/constitution/eng%402010-09-03/source?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**I. Contract performance and benefits realisation**

Procurement value cannot be determined at award.

The system should measure:

- Delivery timeliness
- Quantity and quality accepted
- Service levels
- Defects and corrective actions
- Contract variations
- Price escalation
- Supplier performance
- User satisfaction where appropriate
- Planned versus actual benefits
- Warranty and maintenance performance
- Final contract cost
- Termination, penalties and disputes

The award establishes expected value. Contract Management determines whether it was realised.

**J. Transparency and public accountability**

KenTender should automatically produce appropriate public information from authoritative transaction data.

This could include:

- Procurement plans
- Tender notices and amendments
- Award notices
- Contract summaries
- Procurement method statistics
- Preference and reservation performance
- Contract implementation status
- Disposal results
- Non-sensitive performance indicators

Transparency should be designed around lawful publication rules, confidentiality and data protection-not uncontrolled publication of every internal record.

**K. Professionalism and institutional learning**

The Act also invokes principles governing the procurement profession. The system can help by:

- Assigning activities only to authorised roles
- Recording committee composition and qualifications
- Providing stage-specific legal guidance
- Reusing approved specifications and criteria
- Capturing reasons for failed procurements
- Comparing estimated and actual timelines
- Maintaining lessons learned
- Identifying recurring procedural errors
- Measuring workload and capability gaps

This converts each procurement into institutional learning rather than an isolated file.

**L. Risk and resilience**

Resilience is not expressly presented as a standalone PPADA heading, but it follows from prudent planning, value for money, service effectiveness and contract management.

It can cover:

- Supply continuity
- Critical supplier dependency
- Foreign exchange exposure
- Cybersecurity and data-residency risks
- Availability of maintenance and spare parts
- Disaster and emergency readiness
- Contract concentration
- Supplier financial health
- Business continuity commitments

These controls must be tender-specific, not a universal risk questionnaire.

**3\. Not everything should become a "strategic goal"**

We should distinguish three classes:

| **Class**                     | **Purpose**                                   | **Examples**                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public-value objective        | Result the procurement is expected to achieve | Reduced service downtime, lower lifecycle cost, increased local participation       |
| Mandatory guardrail           | Constraint that must not be violated          | Budget approval, fair competition, conflict controls, published evaluation criteria |
| System-generated intelligence | Insight derived without additional user entry | Cycle time, bidder participation, approval returns, variation rate                  |

This distinction prevents Strategy Alignment from becoming an enormous checklist.

Strategy Alignment should hold selected organisational outcomes. Legal guardrails should be built into workflows. Operational intelligence should be calculated automatically.

**4\. Recommended expanded framework**

I recommend eight top-level pillars for KenTender:

1. **Strategic and service outcomes**
2. **Economy and whole-life value**
3. **Process efficiency**
4. **Fair competition and market access**
5. **Inclusion and economic development**
6. **Sustainability and asset stewardship**
7. **Integrity and accountability**
8. **Contract performance and resilience**

Legal compliance remains the foundation beneath all eight; it is not a ninth, competing objective.

The next task should therefore be to create a **statutory and public-value obligations matrix** before redesigning Strategy Alignment. That matrix should identify:

- Legal or policy source
- Obligation or value
- Applicability
- Lifecycle stage
- Responsible module
- Enforcement mechanism
- Evidence
- Measure
- Reporting destination

That will show precisely which attributes belong in Strategy Alignment, which belong in automatic controls, and which should be produced through analytics.
