KenTender - Public Procurement Value Framework Example

# 1\. Introduction

KenTender must convert the governing principles and operative controls of public procurement into system behaviour so that public value, efficiency, sustainability, inclusion and performance are first-class artefacts, not disconnected questionnaires or retrospective analytics.

The following is an illustrative Ministry of Health fixture. The values are test data, not statutory or policy-prescribed thresholds.

This example shows how KenTender can carry public-procurement principles from strategy through demand, tender configuration, contract commitments, performance measurement, asset handling and management reporting.

# 2\. What the legal framework expects

The governing principles can be organised into the following domains:

| **Domain**                                                                      | **Legal foundation**                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fairness, equity, transparency, competition and cost-effectiveness              | Constitution Article 227                                  |
| National values, inclusion, human dignity and protection of marginalised groups | Constitution Article 10                                   |
| Equality and freedom from discrimination                                        | Constitution Article 27                                   |
| Youth and marginalised-group affirmative action                                 | Constitution Articles 55 and 56                           |
| Integrity and professional conduct                                              | Leadership and Integrity Act and PPADA                    |
| Openness, accountability, public participation and prudent use of public money  | Constitution Article 201                                  |
| Efficient, effective and economic use of resources                              | Constitution Article 232                                  |
| Value for money                                                                 | PPADA section 3                                           |
| Local industry and citizen contractors                                          | PPADA section 3 and preference and reservation provisions |
| Sustainable development and environmental protection                            | PPADA section 3                                           |
| Responsible procurement, inventory, contract and disposal management            | PPADA lifecycle provisions                                |

The constitutional standards are not peripheral references. PPADA expressly makes them governing principles of procurement and disposal.

| PVO-EFY-01 | Efficiency        | Reduce implementation and service-restoration time  | Days to deploy; mean time to restore                    | ICT projects                    |

| PVO-LOC-01 | Local capability  | Develop internal and local technical capability     | Personnel trained and independently assessed            | Specialist technology contracts |
| PVO-SUS-01 | Sustainability    | Reduce infrastructure energy consumption            | Energy consumed at an agreed workload                   | Data-centre equipment           |

These objectives are **available for selection** when they are relevant to a procurement. They are not automatically imposed on every Ministry procurement, because each demand should carry only the public-value objectives that fit its purpose, risk and expected contribution.

# 6\. Example procurement

## Demand

Supply, installation and support of server infrastructure for the Ministry of Health data centre.

## Primary strategic alignment

| **Field**          | **Selection**                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Plan               | MOH-SP-0001                                                                                          |
| Programme          | Digital Health Services                                                                                   |
| Sub-programme      | Health Information Systems                                                                                |
| Outcome            | Reliable and accessible digital clinical services                                                         |
| Indicator          | Availability of core clinical information systems                                                         |
| Target             | At least 99.9% availability by 30 June 2028                                                               |
| Alignment type     | Primary                                                                                                   |
| Reason             | Existing infrastructure contributes to recurrent outages and cannot support the target availability level |
| Contribution owner | Director, Digital Health                                                                                  |

The demand may have additional supporting alignments, but it must have only one primary alignment so that responsibility for the procurement's main strategic contribution remains clear.

# 7\. Procurement Value Case

Only objectives relevant to this procurement are selected.

| **Objective**                                   | **Baseline**                                | **Procurement target**                                                                           | **Proposed treatment**                              | **Benefit owner**        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Improve service availability                    | 97.8%                                       | At least 99.9%                                                                                   | Technical architecture requirement and contract KPI | Director, Digital Health |
| Reduce whole-life infrastructure cost           | KES 120 million over five years             | Not more than KES 105 million                                                                    | Evaluated five-year TCO model                       | Head of ICT Finance      |
| Improve service resilience                      | Recovery time: 12 hours                     | Recovery within 4 hours                                                                          | Mandatory technical requirement and acceptance test | Infrastructure Manager   |
| Develop technical capability                    | 2 personnel able to administer the platform | At least 10 trained; 6 independently assessed as competent                                       | Contract deliverable and KPI                        | Head of ICT Operations   |
| Reduce energy consumption                       | 60 kW at the defined operating load         | Not more than 45 kW at the same agreed load                                                      | Mandatory specification and acceptance test         | Data Centre Manager      |
| Ensure compliant handling of replaced equipment | Disposal route not yet determined           | 100% of replaced equipment accounted for and routed through approved transfer, reuse or disposal | Demand gate, asset control and disposal control     | Head of Asset Management |

The Value Case is approved before detailed tender configuration so that only approved public-value objectives, targets and treatments can flow into the tender design.

# 8\. Demand challenge

Before authorising procurement, the system asks for decisions supported by data:

| **Question**                        | **Example decision**                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can existing equipment be repaired? | Some equipment remains usable but cannot meet the required capacity and resilience                   |
| Can equipment be reassigned?        | Eight servers can be reassigned to non-production environments                                       |
| What will be replaced?              | Twenty-four identified server assets                                                                 |
| Is aggregation possible?            | Server, installation, monitoring and initial support will be procured as one coordinated requirement |
| What happens to displaced assets?   | Eight reassigned, six retained for approved spares and ten assessed for disposal                     |
| Is funding available?               | Confirmed against the approved ICT infrastructure budget                                             |
| Is the demand proportionate?        | Capacity model and utilisation evidence attached                                                     |

This demand challenge prevents unnecessary acquisition by requiring the Ministry to test whether repair, reassignment, aggregation, funding and proportionality have been considered before a new procurement is authorised.

# 9\. Approved downstream treatment

Strategy Alignment does not directly create tender criteria. Following Demand and Planning approval, Tender Configuration receives the approved Value Case.

| **Value objective**     | **Tender treatment**                                                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Availability            | Mandatory architecture response plus contract availability KPI                                        |
| Whole-life cost         | Published evaluated-price formula covering acquisition, support, energy and specified lifecycle costs |
| Resilience              | Mandatory recovery architecture and witnessed acceptance test                                         |
| Technical capability    | Training plan, knowledge-transfer deliverables and competency verification                            |
| Energy consumption      | Published operating-load definition and maximum accepted consumption                                  |
| Replaced assets         | Asset schedule and approved reuse, transfer or disposal workflow                                      |
| Contract-change control | Standard variation controls; reported as management intelligence rather than bidder evaluation        |

The published tender locks these treatments. Strategy objectives cannot later be converted into undisclosed evaluation criteria.

# 10\. Contract commitments

Following award, the selected bid produces contract commitments:

| **Commitment**          | **Contract value**                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Service availability    | 99.95%                                                                  |
| Five-year evaluated TCO | KES 101.5 million                                                       |
| Recovery time           | 3 hours                                                                 |
| Personnel training      | 12 staff                                                                |
| Competency outcome      | At least 8 staff assessed as competent                                  |
| Energy consumption      | Maximum 43 kW at the agreed load                                        |
| Deployment period       | 75 days                                                                 |
| Warranty and support    | 36 months                                                               |
| Replaced equipment      | All 24 assets accounted for through reassignment, retention or disposal |

The system preserves both:

- The Ministry's approved target
- The supplier's awarded commitment

They are not the same record.
